# Close known gaps while GPUs screen novelty

**The novel ideas most likely to add Elo to ChessSI attack its two binding constraints, the noisy game-result label and the CPU cost of SPRTs. The five best are untried in NNUE alpha-beta chess, cheap to screen, and testable on ChessSI's own data.** They are: branched ("vine") self-play that gives a shared game prefix the mean result of two continuations (from TRPO and KataGo; central guess +8 Elo); a stop-gradient that lets the game result train only the output layer (from KataGo; +3); an HL-Gauss histogram auxiliary head (from distributional reinforcement learning; +5); exact a↔h mirroring of castling-free positions (from AlphaGo Zero; +4 to +6); and an ensemble of ChessSI's own wider networks as a training-only teacher (+5). All five extend the mechanism behind ChessSI's one proven novel idea, a cleaner second target for the shared layer, which won +53 and +35 Elo, and all but the vine can be screened on the idle GPU before a single game is played. Two testing methods could matter more than any single idea, because those estimates fall where an SPRT is slowest: in this research's simulations a complementary-pair ("foldover") factorial design decided four small ideas in 2.1× fewer game pairs and seven in 2.5× fewer, and raising SPRT beta from 0.05 to 0.2 returned about 1.6× more Elo per CPU hour. The CPU's games should still go first to Known gaps that measured +6 to +36 Elo each at ChessSI's time control in Stash and Alexandria: time management, a transposition-table overhaul, correction history, singular extensions and the history family. The novel search ideas (correction history keyed by the network's own activations, log-calibrated pruning margins, randomised pruning audits, a learned uncertainty head) can only be credited honestly on top of those. Six ideas in ChessSI's log need new prior-art labels, chiefly GPU-teacher relabelling and weighting positions by abs(static eval − search score), which Viridithas, Stockfish, YaneuraOu and Lc0's "diff focus" already do. "Novel" means not found in the code of nnue-pytorch, bullet, the Lc0 and KataGo trainers and about ten open engines, or in web searches; TalkChess, arXiv and most chessprogramming wiki pages were seen only through search snippets, and Discord not at all. Every Elo range is a prior anchored on ChessSI's own effect sizes rather than a measurement.

## Result noise and SPRT cost bound every idea

**ChessSI's only proven novel idea shows where the next ones lie.** The training-only auxiliary head won **+53.2 ± 15.6 Elo** and replicated at **+34.6 ± 12.2**, and its ablations isolate the mechanism. A head predicting the position's own search score, zero plies ahead, matched the 4-ply head (validation loss 0.00652 against 0.00653; no head scored 0.00662). A head predicting only the game result was far worse at 0.00717, and a policy head hurt at every weight tried ([ChessSI IDEAS.md](https://github.com/LupeHiguera/ChessSI/blob/main/docs/IDEAS.md)). The exported output learns 0.75·σ(score/400) + 0.25·result ([train.py](https://github.com/LupeHiguera/ChessSI/blob/main/nnue/train.py)), so the auxiliary head helps by giving the 256 shared units a second target without the result's noise. That is the pattern multi-task learning predicts for tasks that share signal but not noise ([Caruana 1997](https://www.cs.williams.edu/~andrea/cs374/Articles/Caruana.pdf)). The noisy part cannot simply be dropped: training with no game result (`--lambda 1.0`) lost **−80.4 ± 18.7**, while giving the result more weight (`--lambda 0.6`) was level. The result carries information the search lacks, yet it is also ChessSI's noisiest label, because **net9's 119.8 million positions come from only 1.44 million games** ([nets/README.md](https://github.com/LupeHiguera/ChessSI/blob/main/nets/README.md)). About 83 positions share each win, draw or loss. Almost every promising training idea below keeps the result's information while cutting its noise.

**Data volume is still the strongest lever, and network size is not yet the limit.** Computed from the log, each doubling of data has been worth roughly **75 to 140 Elo**. net6 gained about +85 over net4 for 2.2× the data, once the auxiliary head's +53 is set aside; net7 gained +103.7 for 1.7×, net8 +43.1 for 1.4×, and net9's +48.4 ± 14.3 for 1.31× comes to about 124 Elo per doubling ([IDEAS.md](https://github.com/LupeHiguera/ChessSI/blob/main/docs/IDEAS.md), [nets/README.md](https://github.com/LupeHiguera/ChessSI/blob/main/nets/README.md)). These figures overstate pure quantity, since each step also added data from a stronger engine, but width offers far less. h384 and h512 validated only slightly better than h256 at 64.6M positions while h512 searches about 40% slower, and eight material output buckets were level at −0.7 ± 14.7. Ideas that add exact labels at no CPU cost (mirroring) or cleaner labels at no extra search (vine averaging) therefore have unusual leverage. So does a Known fix: **37.2 million generation-1 positions scored by the pre-NNUE PeSTO engine still make up 31% of net9's data**, weighted equally with everything else.

**Testing cost is the other wall.** ChessSI's `sprt.sh` already runs the state-of-the-art statistic, fastchess's default normalized-Elo GSPRT on pentanomial game pairs, which fishtest and OpenBench also use ([fastchess sprt.cpp](https://github.com/Disservin/fastchess/blob/master/app/src/matchmaking/sprt/sprt.cpp), [fishtest LLRcalc.py](https://github.com/official-stockfish/fishtest/blob/master/server/fishtest/stats/LLRcalc.py)). ChessSI's [0, 5] bounds are therefore in normalized Elo (nElo); one ChessSI run reported +31.4 Elo as +44.6 nElo, a ratio near 1.4 ([IDEAS.md](https://github.com/LupeHiguera/ChessSI/blob/main/docs/IDEAS.md)). The log shows the price. +43.1 was decided in 1,208 games and +22.2 in 2,212, but a +6.0 ± 4.7 test was still undecided after 9,398 games and a −2.7 test took 11,690 games to fail. This research's Brownian simulation of the test gives an expected **13,000 game pairs for a null idea, 21,500 at the 2.5 nElo midpoint and 4,800 for a 10 nElo idea**. An idea worth +3 to +10 Elo, which covers most candidates below, therefore costs 6,000 to 30,000 games. The GPU, by contrast, trains net9 from scratch in 27 minutes ([nets/README.md](https://github.com/LupeHiguera/ChessSI/blob/main/nets/README.md)).

**That asymmetry sets the screening rule.** In ChessSI's two matched auxiliary-head pairs, each 0.00001 of validation-loss improvement was worth about +4.5 Elo (−0.00011 gave +53, −0.00008 gave +35). That suggests reserving SPRT games for training changes that beat their control by **at least 0.00002 on the stable per-game validation split**, about +9 Elo at that rate. The rule is a heuristic, not a law: output buckets improved validation by 0.00003 and gained nothing in games, and validation loss is not comparable across target blends such as different λ. Search ideas get the same treatment from offline gates computed on logged searches, so a dead idea costs minutes of CPU rather than hours of SPRT.

**Two caveats apply throughout.** The prior-art checks read the current source of nnue-pytorch, bullet, Stockfish, the Lc0 and KataGo trainers, Reckless, PlentyChess, Viridithas, Stormphrax, YaneuraOu and Alexandria, plus Stash's changelog, fishtest's code and web searches. TalkChess, arXiv and most chessprogramming wiki pages were blocked and seen only as search snippets, and fishtest's experiment history and the engine-development Discord servers, where much NNUE work first appears, could not be searched at all. "Novel" therefore means not found in those sources. "Partially known" means the mechanism has a precedent in Go, Lc0, older chess research or machine learning, but was not found in NNUE alpha-beta chess; both count as untried transplants for this report's purpose. And all Elo is self-play Elo at 10+0.1, which overstates gains against other engines: net8 and SEE pruning won +43 and +22 in self-play but +36 together against the Stash gauntlet that produced ChessSI's **3176 ± 29** rating ([IDEAS.md](https://github.com/LupeHiguera/ChessSI/blob/main/docs/IDEAS.md)).

## The best novel bets clean ChessSI's training labels

Fourteen training and data candidates survived the prior-art checks. The table ranks them by expected Elo per unit of CPU, with novelty breaking ties. Every one uses only ChessSI's own games, searches and networks.

**Branched self-play is the largest bet and the most clearly novel.** At a random middlegame ply (uniform over 16-100), datagen forks the game into two continuations and plays each to the end. Every position is still searched and recorded once, but the prefix positions take the mean of the two results as their result target. The design borrows TRPO's "vine" estimator, which runs several rollouts from shared states to cut variance ([Schulman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.05477)), and KataGo's forks, which branch games for diversity but never average the main game's targets ([KataGo selfplay1.cfg](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/cpp/configs/training/selfplay1.cfg)). No forking or result averaging was found in the datagen code of Viridithas, Stormphrax, PlentyChess, Stockfish's tools, nnue-pytorch or bullet. When two branches end differently often enough, averaging halves the after-fork noise of every prefix label at zero extra search. That is a second route to the cleaner target that made the auxiliary head work.

The gate costs about 20 core-minutes. From 2,000 positions at plies 20-80 of existing datagen games, play two continuations each (TT cleared, 5,000 against 5,050 nodes), and stop if fewer than about 10% of pairs end differently. If the probe passes, generate three equal-CPU datasets (control, vine with averaging, vine without). Train each on the old data plus the new set, and let the averaged-minus-unaveraged contrast isolate the mechanism before one SPRT. Use the root position's key for the validation split so that a shared prefix never straddles it, and keep the auxiliary head's "N plies later" lookup within one branch. The same averaging applies at no cost to the 10.19% of positions that deduplication now removes ([IDEAS.md](https://github.com/LupeHiguera/ChessSI/blob/main/docs/IDEAS.md)); today each of those keeps one game's result.

**Gradient routing is the cheapest novel test, and it doubles as a diagnostic.** Writing the loss as λ·MSE(σ(W·x), σ(score)) + (1−λ)·MSE(σ(W·sg(x)), result) changes who learns from the result. Here sg stops the gradient at the accumulator activations x, so the game result trains only the output layer's 513 weights, while the search score and the auxiliary heads shape the feature transformer. Without the stop-gradient the gradient equals today's, so validation on the usual blend stays directly comparable. The isolation trick is KataGo's, which trains its error heads behind a stop-gradient so they cannot change the value head ([KataGoMethods.md](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/docs/KataGoMethods.md)). Lc0's trainer has a `propagate_value_gradients` flag ([training_config.proto](https://github.com/LeelaChessZero/lczero-training/blob/master/proto/training_config.proto)). Neither applies it to the outcome part of a value target, and nnue-pytorch and bullet have nothing like it. About 15 trainer lines and runs with β, the share of the result's gradient that reaches the shared layer, at 0 and 0.5 settle it. If β = 0 loses, the result's value lies in features it teaches the first layer, which points effort toward vine averaging and λ-returns. If β = 0 wins, the result mostly calibrates.

**An HL-Gauss head is the most direct upgrade of the mechanism that already passed.** The head is a training-only linear layer that reads the 2H accumulator values and outputs about 51 bins over win probability. It is trained by cross-entropy against a Gaussian-smoothed histogram of a clean target such as σ(own score/400), and dropped at export. Farebrother et al.'s ICML 2024 oral found that HL-Gauss beat two-hot targets and degraded more slowly than MSE as target noise grew, across Atari, robotics, Wordle and searchless chess ([arXiv 2403.03950](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03950)). Imani and White trace histogram losses' gains to better-behaved gradients ([ICML 2018](https://mlanthology.org/icml/2018/imani2018icml-improving)). That matters here because with MSE on a sigmoid the gradient (σ − t)·σ′ nearly vanishes for clearly won or lost positions, while a softmax cross-entropy gradient does not.

The nearest chess precedent is Lc0's optional categorical value head, which uses plain one-hot buckets and is left out of Lc0's export format ([value_head.py](https://github.com/LeelaChessZero/lczero-training/blob/master/src/lczero_training/model/value_head.py), [jax_to_leela.py](https://github.com/LeelaChessZero/lczero-training/blob/master/src/lczero_training/convert/jax_to_leela.py)). A TalkChess thread found scalar cross-entropy on the main NNUE output no better than MSE, but that is a different design ([TalkChess, snippet](https://talkchess.com/viewtopic.php?t=85963)). Try head weights of 0.003, 0.01 and 0.03, since cross-entropy runs at 1-4 nats against MSE's 0.0065, both beside and in place of the 4-ply MSE head. Watch for the failure mode that sank the policy head, a cross-entropy head swamping 256 units.

**Mirror augmentation is the cheapest data multiplier.** Without castling rights, chess rules are exactly symmetric under the a↔h mirror. Every position whose castling field is "-" can therefore be flipped at random during training (square s to s ^ 7, en-passant square included), with its score and result unchanged. AlphaGo Zero trained on all eight board symmetries, but AlphaZero did not augment chess because its rules are asymmetric ([Silver et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01815)). No one appears to have used the symmetric castling-free subset: nnue-pytorch, bullet, Viridithas and Stormphrax have no augmentation, and NNUE uses mirroring only in its input encoding. Mirrored copies share their originals' label noise, so the gain is well below doubling that subset. Count the castling-free share first, since under about 40% leaves little to gain. Then train with and without flips on the same seed; validation on unflipped games stays comparable. The augmentation substitutes for the Known horizontally mirrored inputs, which give a position and its mirror identical inputs, so it should be tested first: it costs nothing at inference.

**An ensemble teacher is the cleanest target ChessSI can build from its own data.** Train four h512 networks with different seeds on the training split only, average their sigmoid outputs, and use that average as the target of the existing training-only `--teacher` head. Distilling one larger own-data network is Known. Viridithas's "acolyte" net gained +3.69 ± 2.35 at 40+0.4 from "a much larger teacher net", and PlentyChess trains partly by self-distillation ([viridithas-networks releases](https://github.com/cosmobobak/viridithas-networks/releases), [PlentyChess README](https://github.com/Yoshie2000/PlentyChess/blob/9ca005740a35967c1403bfb9a3a9032c97ee003d/README.md)). An ensemble of the engine's own nets as a training-only head target was not found. ChessSI's born-again head moved validation only from 0.00674 to 0.00671, because a teacher's errors match its student's. Wider nets averaged across seeds should be less correlated, and h512 already validated at 0.00649 against h256's 0.00653. A single h512 teacher is the right control, because it separates the ensemble's own contribution from the Known recipe.

**Four further ideas change how the result and the search's bounds enter the target, and they cost more to judge.** A plies-to-end weight w(d) ∝ exp(−d/τ) + c, with mean 0.25, gives the result more say near the end of a game, where it is nearly decided. A λ-return, G_t = (1−μ)·σ(s_{t+1}) + μ·G_{t+1}, instead replaces distant results with the consensus of later searches, as TD(λ), KnightCap's TDLeaf and KataGo's short-term value targets do ([KnightCap](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/9901002), [KataGoMethods.md](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/docs/KataGoMethods.md)). nnue-pytorch and bullet schedule λ only over training time ([nnue-pytorch config.py](https://github.com/official-stockfish/nnue-pytorch/blob/master/model/config.py)). A free gate exists: held-out games show whether σ(score) predicts the result better near the end, and a flat curve kills the idea. But validation loss is not comparable across targets, so each variant needs games, unless ChessSI first builds a gold validation set of about 200,000 held-out positions searched at about 200,000 nodes, roughly 4×10^10 nodes.

Mid-game exploration with deblundered results makes ChessSI's deterministic post-opening games visit positions off the main line. After ply 16, with probability about 0.04 per move, it plays a root move within about 40 cp of the best. Walking backwards as Lc0's deblunder does, it then replaces the result before any costly exploration move with the best move's searched value ([Tilps/lc0 loop.cc](https://github.com/Tilps/lc0/blob/rescore_tb/src/selfplay/loop.cc)). Stockfish's generator explores with MultiPV in the first 24 plies but never repairs results ([generate_training_data.md](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/tools/docs/generate_training_data.md)). ChessSI first needs the excluded-move search that singular extensions will add.

RHO-LOSS trains on positions whose current loss exceeds an "irreducible" loss estimated by networks that never saw them; here, two half-data h256 nets would score each other's halves. It reached 18× fewer training steps on the noisy Clothing-1M dataset ([Mindermann et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07137)). Data-pruning theory says to keep easy examples while data is scarce ([Sorscher et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14486)), so RHO-LOSS belongs after the second PC's data jump. A one-sided hinge on root siblings' fail-low bounds transplants TreeStrap(αβ), which rated about 1,100 points above TD-Leaf for a linear evaluation in Meep ([CPW Meep](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Meep), [Veness et al.](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/3722-bootstrapping-from-game-tree-search)). Survey the depth at which fail-low root moves were searched first, since bounds from moves reduced below depth 4 are too weak to use.

**The tail is weaker, and some famous transplants do not fit at all.** Iteration-score heads are novel but promise about +2 and need new data. SAM, untried in NNUE, shines mainly in over-parameterised, noisy settings ([Baek et al.](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2024/poster/19504)). Reweighting toward evaluated positions, prioritised start states and selective deep relabelling all need equal-CPU datasets. Braun's preprint is a warning for the last two: a learned search-allocation selector raised label agreement yet trailed controls by 60-100 Elo ([arXiv 2609.37447](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.37447v1)). Gumbel AlphaZero's policy improvement has no policy to improve in a value-only network. KataGo's power-law replay window, computed from its formula, would keep only the newest 7.2M of net8's 91.3M rows ([shuffle.py](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/python/shuffle.py)). A colour-flip consistency loss does nothing, because the perspective network already shares weights across colours.

## Two design tricks could double or triple decisions per CPU hour

**The public testing frameworks share one statistic and lack the same things.** fishtest, OpenBench and fastchess all use Van den Bergh's normalized-Elo GSPRT on pentanomial pairs. fishtest adds STC and LTC presets, classic SPSA and median-unbiased Elo after a test stops ([fishtest sprt.py](https://github.com/official-stockfish/fishtest/blob/master/server/fishtest/stats/sprt.py), [OpenBench stats.py](https://github.com/AndyGrant/OpenBench/blob/master/OpenBench/stats.py)). None implements decision-theoretic stopping, factorial designs, racing among variants or opening selection by the tested engine, and those gaps hold the candidates. Their payoff compounds with every other section of this report, because the novel ideas' central estimates of +2 to +8 Elo fall where the SPRT is slowest.

**Expected-loss stopping spends fewer games on the ideas whose decision matters least.** Instead of waiting for the log-likelihood ratio to cross ±2.94, the test stops once the posterior expected loss of the better decision falls below a "threshold of caring". That is the rule Bayesian A/B testing uses ([Stucchio](https://www.chrisstucchio.com/blog/2014/bayesian_ab_decision_rule.html)), with the prior on effects fitted here to ChessSI's own log. Chess forums have noted that SPRT bounds imply a prior ([open-chess](https://open-chess.org/viewtopic.php?p=19612)), but no framework implements such a rule, so it is Partially known. The zero-code first step is `BETA=0.2`, which `sprt.sh` already reads from the environment ([sprt.sh](https://github.com/LupeHiguera/ChessSI/blob/main/scripts/sprt.sh)). This research simulated both rules with the same Gaussian pair model:

| Prior on true effects (nElo) | SPRT [0, 5], beta 0.05 | SPRT [0, 5], beta 0.2 | Stop when expected loss < 0.2 nElo |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| N(0, 6²) | 2.05 | 3.32 (1.62×) | 3.85 (1.88×) |
| N(2, 8²) | 4.32 | 6.79 (1.57×) | 9.55 (2.21×) |
| N(5, 15²) | 14.5 | 22.8 (1.57×) | 39.0 (2.69×) |

*nElo gained per 10,000 game pairs, net of harmful accepts (this research's simulation).*

Beta 0.2 added no measurable harmful accepts. The expected-loss rule costs about 0.1 nElo per idea in them, and a threshold of 0.05 nElo did worse than the SPRT. The simulation also knew the prior exactly, which a prior fitted to about 30 logged tests will not. The experiment itself is free: replay each saved SPRT's games through both rules in PGN order, fit the prior on half the tests and score the rule on the other half, then shadow-run it on new tests. The change alters the benchmark's error rates, its hit rate and its games per accepted change, so it needs the owner's approval. It should run with the LLR logged alongside, plus a periodic non-regression SPRT against an older checkpoint to catch small losses that accumulate.

**A complementary-pair factorial gives every game to every factor.** Three to seven independent changes go behind UCI switches in one binary. Every game pair plays a configuration against its exact complement, with the switch settings cycled through an 8-run orthogonal array, and each factor gets its own pentanomial GSPRT on the pair outcomes. Because s_j·s_l is unchanged when every sign flips, two-factor interactions cancel out of each pair's score difference. Each factor's estimate then has the variance of a solo head-to-head test on the same pairs. H.G. Muller's "orthogonal multi-testing" of about 2009 instead played all 2^k configurations through a common gauntlet, which compares engines indirectly and costs about four times the games ([TalkChess, snippet](https://talkchess.com/viewtopic.php?p=271592)). The head-to-head complement version with per-factor sequential tests was not found. In this research's simulation, four factors with effects of (4.3, 7.1, 0, −2.8) nElo were all decided in **2.06× fewer pairs** than four separate SPRTs, and seven factors in **2.54× fewer**, with matching pass rates.

The risks are ChessSI's own. Overlapping techniques interact strongly: history went from −2.7 to +60.4 once LMR was in, and RFP shrank from +68 to +31.4 after futility pruning ([IDEAS.md](https://github.com/LupeHiguera/ChessSI/blob/main/docs/IDEAS.md)). Batches should therefore mix only changes from different subsystems, keep the pair count equal across runs (in simulation, an unbalanced schedule raised a 2-nElo factor's pass rate from 0.35 to 0.73) and end with one confirming SPRT of the accepted bundle. A positive control comes first, as `IDEAS.md` already requires. Toggle three accepted, unrelated features plus a dummy switch for about 3,000 pairs, which gives each factor a 95% interval of about ±9 nElo. Check that the dummy reads zero and that the others match their solo results in sign and rough size; the solo results came from older baselines, so only a rough match is expected.

**Two smaller levers are cheap.** The first is selecting openings by the tested engine's own eval, the principled form of ChessSI's lead that unbalanced openings carry more signal. In this research's retrospective of 1,027 pairs, pair-score variance fell from 0.305 in the most balanced band to 0.211 at 1.0-1.25 pawns, while CPU per pair stayed flat at 56-60 seconds. That alone would give about 1.45× signal per pair, close to the 1.46× ChessSI measured at 0.8-1.0 pawns ([IDEAS.md](https://github.com/LupeHiguera/ChessSI/blob/main/docs/IDEAS.md)). The band should be computed with the base engine at fixed depth before each test, never with the new engine. Choose it by cross-validation across past tests and recompute it after every network change; expect 22-32% fewer pairs. UHO already selects openings by eval window using other engines' evaluations ([UHO 2024](https://www.sp-cc.de/uho_2024.htm)), so this is Partially known. The second lever is racing, for questions with several discrete variants such as λ sweeps: play the variants against each other, eliminate losers at interim looks, and confirm the survivor with one SPRT. It should save 30-50% of games; multi-arm trials saved 37% with four arms against one control ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/h/spr/sprchp/978-3-319-52636-2_110.html)), though racing was not simulated here. Porting fishtest's median-unbiased estimator costs hours and keeps logged Elo for early-stopped tests from being biased away from zero.

**Four statistical ideas should not be pursued.** CUPED-style adjustment on opening features gains nearly nothing. It helps only through covariates that predict the outcome, and colour-reversed pairs already remove the opening's main effect ([CUPED](https://exp-platform.com/cuped/)). Lorden's 2-SPRT saves 12% at the midpoint but costs 25-41% more games on 7.5-15 nElo winners in simulation ([survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.18782)). Using the 2+0.02 pre-screen as a prior fails as well: over the 11 ideas with both results, the pre-screens correlate with the 10+0.1 results at r = 0.81, yet imply about 24 Elo of genuine disagreement, more than today's effect sizes. Debiased truncation applies prediction-powered inference and AIPW to score games from their early positions ([Angelopoulos et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09633)). It is novel in chess but retrospectively negative. Two-phase sampling gains only when the truncated prefix's CPU share c is below ρ², the squared correlation between the surrogate and the final pair score, and on ChessSI's games c exceeds ρ² at every ply (0.41 against 0.09 at ply 20, 0.80 against 0.55 at ply 60). It should be logged as a failed novel methodology after the retrospective is rerun on all 32,743 games.

## Five Known gaps outweigh any single novel bet

**The largest, surest Elo left is Known search work, and it is also the foundation the novel search ideas need.** Stash and Alexandria measured the gains below at 8+0.08 or 10+0.1, the same time-control class as ChessSI's tests, and Stash 27-36 is the pool ChessSI is rated against. For ChessSI, the top five ranges sum to +60 to +125 before overlap, more than any novel idea's upside. They also unlock later work. The TT overhaul enables singular extensions and ProbCut, correction history enables abs(correction) margins and two of the novel search ideas, history kept between moves enables continuation history and move priors, and time management enables a learned stop rule. ChessSI's own log shows why the order matters: results change sign with their baseline, as when history went from −2.7 to +60.4 and late move pruning from −140 to +52.5. A novel idea tested on the bare engine would be credited with gains the Known feature would have brought anyway.

**Several Known features already failed in ChessSI, for fixable reasons.** Probing the TT in quiescence lost −19.7 ± 19.3 in its pre-screen. Probe-and-store was stopped at −18.3 ± 26.1, because same-key stores overwrote deeper entries and probing without prefetch cost 26% of nodes per second. The fix starts with replacement by depth and age, whose test stands at +4.4 ± 7.9 after 3,660 games and was handed to the PC on `claude/cloud-tt-replacement` ([branch log](https://github.com/LupeHiguera/ChessSI/blob/claude/cloud-tt-replacement/docs/IDEAS.md)). It continues with depth-0 qsearch entries that never evict deeper ones, probed with prefetch. Keeping history between moves without decay lost −9.9 ± 18.5, so halve it at each `go` instead. Continuation history lost −2.9 ± 18.1 while its tables reset every move, so retest it once history persists. Check evasions in quiescence lost −11.8 ± 8.4 on an older baseline ([IDEAS.md](https://github.com/LupeHiguera/ChessSI/blob/main/docs/IDEAS.md)). The queued idea of ordering losing captures below quiets has mixed evidence: +17.9 in Stash, but +1.47 ± 4.25 and no pass in Alexandria ([2a3d37a](https://github.com/PGG106/Alexandria/commit/2a3d37a77df011c5fdd926d4e93f1f0d5eb79c47)).

**On the network side, speed comes before size.** `nnue.cpp` already forms v·w in int16 before multiplying by v, the SCReLU trick the queue still lists. But it runs auto-vectorised loops over a runtime hidden size and updates both perspectives eagerly on every move ([nnue.cpp](https://github.com/LupeHiguera/ChessSI/blob/main/src/nnue.cpp)). The Known path combines a compile-time hidden size, explicit AVX2 and AVX-VNNI intrinsics, register-tiled updates and lazy accumulator updates of the kind Stockfish uses ([nnue_accumulator.h](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/effdc3fd3f6de6e75fe1374a51a7a7eed66f880b/src/nnue/nnue_accumulator.h)). Tetrarch's lazy per-perspective updates alone gave +59.8% nodes per second ([Tetrarch releases](https://github.com/IchNukeDichWeg/Tetrarch/releases)). Suppose h512's 40% slowdown means the network takes about 40% of node time. At the 50-70 Elo per speed doubling that two-player engines typically see (the range Tetrarch's release notes quote), this research's cost model then says a network twice as fast is worth roughly +16 to +23 Elo. It also says h512 needs +24 to +34 Elo of evaluation quality just to break even. Verify the share with `perf`, and measure ChessSI's own exchange rate once with a time-odds match.

After speed, the Known order follows the data. Horizontal mirroring and a retest of output buckets come now. Four to eight mirrored king buckets with a factoriser and Finny tables come at 0.3-1 billion positions; Leorik's buckets gained about +20 Elo despite about −10% speed ([TalkChess, snippet](https://www.talkchess.com/viewtopic.php?p=987228)). More width comes once inference is fast, and a second layer only past about 1 billion positions, as bullet's guide advises ([bullet docs](https://github.com/jw1912/bullet/blob/579b78b37a694cacd6e9ede4706386c15c34837c/docs/1-basics.md)). The single-layer ceiling is high: Leorik reached CCRL Blitz 3496 with one hidden layer of 640, five king buckets and eight output buckets ([Leorik README](https://github.com/lithander/Leorik)).

**Training and datagen have Known fixes that cost GPU time or a datagen restart, not games.** On the training side, ChessSI can down-weight or anneal away the PeSTO generation, as Stockfish's staged retraining does ([PR #4465](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/pull/4465), [nnue-pytorch wiki](https://github.com/official-stockfish/nnue-pytorch/wiki/Training-datasets)). It can export an EMA or SWA of the weights instead of the last Adam iterate, as Lc0 can ([training_config.proto](https://github.com/LeelaChessZero/lczero-training/blob/master/proto/training_config.proto)), and try Ranger with Lookahead, which gained Viridithas +8.98 ± 3.92 over Adam ([viridithas-networks releases](https://github.com/cosmobobak/viridithas-networks/releases)). It should also measure the float-to-int16 eval error before considering the quantisation-aware training nnue-pytorch runs by default ([config.py](https://github.com/official-stockfish/nnue-pytorch/blob/master/model/config.py)). ChessSI's datagen differs from the own-data engines in four Known ways ([viridithas datagen.rs](https://github.com/cosmobobak/viridithas/blob/b407e3eb0b8fc6e251d22664b696cfa870208a7a/src/datagen.rs), [Stormphrax datagen.cpp](https://github.com/Ciekce/Stormphrax/blob/d1468d99e3d3733100d20d682ac627998cce94b2/src/datagen/datagen.cpp), [ChessSI datagen.cpp](https://github.com/LupeHiguera/ChessSI/blob/main/src/datagen.cpp)). It searches 5,000 hard nodes a move, where Viridithas uses 25,000 soft nodes with an 8× hard cap. It never adjudicates draws, which Viridithas does at abs(score) ≤ 4 for 12 plies. It runs no verification search on its random openings, while Viridithas discards openings beyond 1,000 cp after a 50,000-node search and Stormphrax those beyond 500 cp at depth 10. And it filters positions as it writes them instead of storing whole games. Storing whole games is the enabler for the vine, for λ-returns, and for training-time filters such as Stockfish's `wld_filtered` and piece-count reshaping ([training_data_loader.cpp](https://github.com/official-stockfish/nnue-pytorch/blob/ed84cc1fd2255faa0f33dcfa7839c9b6b4bb49b8/data_loader/cpp/training_data_loader.cpp)). The 5,000-versus-10,000-node question can wait until about 0.5-1 billion positions: at 75-140 Elo per doubling of data, halving the positions to get better labels cannot pay yet.

## Novel search and architecture ideas need those foundations

### Most search transplants already exist in chess under other names

**Optimisation search and MCTS mostly rediscovered what alpha-beta engines already do.** SAT solvers' conflict-history branching uses the same exponential recency-weighted average as ChessSI's history gravity ([CHB, AAAI 2016](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/10439)), and clause learning maps to TT entries, killers and correction history. Planning's online heuristic-error learning ([Thayer et al., ICAPS 2011](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/view/13474)) is correction history, which reached Stockfish from Caissa in 2023 ([b4d995d](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/commit/b4d995d0d910044cf4ea2ad3ee30fd1d21070cd8)). KataGo's pattern-keyed subtree value bias, worth +30 to +60 Elo there ([KataGoMethods.md](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/docs/KataGoMethods.md)), is a correction history with a different key. Shogi's realization-probability search is close to LMR and to Giraffe's probability-limited search ([Winands & Björnsson](https://staff.ru.is/yngvi/pdf/WinandsB08a.pdf), [Giraffe](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01549)), and YaneuraOu's search is a close port of Stockfish's ([yaneuraou-search.cpp](https://github.com/yaneurao/YaneuraOu/blob/bd6db90a7ac91875356f6e4ac61a18c9031a9116/source/engine/yaneuraou-engine/yaneuraou-search.cpp)). Two transplants fail on mechanism. Gumbel MuZero's sequential halving at the root ([ICLR 2022](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2022/poster/6418)) duplicates what null windows and root LMR already do while risking the costliest misses (expected −10 to +3). Planning's novelty pruning has nothing to prune in a dense evaluation. Four mechanisms had no chess twin: keying search-learned corrections by the evaluator's own representation; a learned uncertainty output used inside alpha-beta; MIP-style reliability gating between cheap statistics and expensive probes ([Achterberg et al.](https://opus4.kobv.de/opus4-zib/frontdoor/index/index/docId/788)); and statistically calibrated, randomly audited pruning.

**ChessSI's economics make offline-learned search control rational again.** Chess research tried learned move ordering, probability-driven depth and learned time allocation, and modern engines abandoned them ([Kocsis et al.](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/move-ordering-using-neural-networks), [Giraffe](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01549)). Those engines learn online with histories and tune constants with SPSA, because online tables adapt to the actual position and SPSA optimises the real objective. Offline fitting wins only when it is much cheaper per decision, which is exactly ChessSI's position: logging and fitting cost minutes of CPU, GPU time is spare, and games are the bottleneck. The claim to test for these ideas is therefore "the same Elo for far fewer games". One design rule applies to all of them. Labels from the engine's own searches are biased by its current move ordering and pruning, so a small share of nodes should be sampled with the relevant pruning switched off.

### Four search ideas are new, and each has an offline gate

| Idea (source field) | Verdict and nearest prior art | Expected Elo | Needs first | Offline gate before games |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

**Pruning audits come first because they cost no Elo and produce research data.** With probability about 1/256, a node that RFP, futility, LMP or null-move pruning would cut is searched anyway and the outcome logged. The audits sit behind a UCI option and do not update history, killers or correction tables. Verified null-move pruning re-searches every null-move cutoff to catch zugzwang ([Tabibi & Netanyahu](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/0808.1125)), and engine authors report how often each pruning fires, but no random-sample measurement of false-prune rates was found. A thousand logged games at 10+0.1 give per-technique, per-depth error rates. They also give the unbiased labels for log-calibrated margins. Today's margins are RFP's 150·d up to depth 3, futility's 120·d up to depth 6 and the null-move gate ([search.cpp](https://github.com/LupeHiguera/ChessSI/blob/main/src/search.cpp)); calibration replaces them with the (1 − ε) quantile of (search value − static eval) by depth and cheap features, with ε near 1%. ProbCut and Multi-ProbCut set cut thresholds from a regression's residual σ, and Multi-ProbCut scored 59.4% in a 64-game chess match ([Jiang & Buro](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~jiang/papers/mpc_main.pdf)). Stockfish and Reckless widen their margins by abs(correction) ([Stockfish search.cpp](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/effdc3fd3f6de6e75fe1374a51a7a7eed66f880b/src/search.cpp), [Reckless search.rs](https://github.com/codedeliveryservice/Reckless/blob/69aea9086899189ee38fd3e556bf9469b805552a/src/search.rs)). The calibration is therefore Partially known. What is new is fitting static-pruning margins to the engine's own residual quantiles instead of tuning them by SPSA, which gained Alexandria +21.14 ± 9.13 ([9f81a27](https://github.com/PGG106/Alexandria/commit/9f81a272d936811710e19d92bc1f785e3e5a5d5f)). ChessSI's margins predate its NNUE, and its RFP stops at depth 3, while Stockfish's runs below depth 19. The gain over today's margins could be +5 to +20 with moderate confidence, of which perhaps 0 to +5 would survive against an SPSA-tuned baseline.

**Representation-keyed correction history is the most novel search idea.** Alongside the pawn and non-pawn keys, it hashes the signs of 12-20 chosen hidden units of the side-to-move accumulator into a correction table of 16-64K entries, updated by the usual rule. A bias the search discovers then generalises along the network's own notion of similarity, which KataGo's pattern-keyed bias suggests and locality-sensitive hashing makes cheap. Every correction key found in Stockfish, Reckless, PlentyChess, Sirius and Viridithas is a hand-chosen piece set, pawn structure or move history ([PlentyChess history.h](https://github.com/Yoshie2000/PlentyChess/blob/9ca005740a35967c1403bfb9a3a9032c97ee003d/src/history.h), [Stockfish 60351b9](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/commit/60351b9df901ff5278f208a9cf3a40059ff54832)). Stockfish's three extra keys together came to about +4.4 at STC and +8.7 at LTC, computed from that commit's results, so expect +0 to +6 on top of Known correction history. The gate is offline. Log unit signs, corrected evals and search values at depth ≥ 2 nodes, replay the update rule, and continue only if the accumulator key cuts residual variance clearly more than a random-bit key of the same width. That control exposes the effect that any extra key helps a little.

**A stop-gradient uncertainty head sharpens the queued uncertainty-aware evaluation.** The head is a second network output that predicts abs(search score − static eval). It is trained behind a stop-gradient so it cannot hurt the value. It can widen pruning margins, soften LMR, size aspiration windows and, at the root, scale the soft time limit. KataGo reported about +75 Elo for its learned uncertainty, used together with variance-scaled exploration and not isolated. An Lc0 fork reports +20 at long time control, unverified ([KataGoMethods.md](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/docs/KataGoMethods.md), [Ergodice/lc0](https://github.com/Ergodice/lc0)). But the alpha-beta baseline to beat is abs(correction), which Stockfish and Reckless already feed into RFP, futility, singular margins and LMR. The gate is the partial correlation between predicted and actual eval error after controlling for abs(correction) and phase. Datagen must first record the static eval.

**The remaining search transplants are smaller or overlap Known features.** A logistic stop rule fitted to iteration logs (best-move changes, score drops, node share, aspiration failures) generalises Kocsis et al.'s learned time allocation in Lines of Action ([Maastricht](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/learning-time-allocation-using-neural-networks)). It should be measured only against Known time management. A Bradley-Terry prior over quiet-move features, after Coulom's Go move patterns ([Coulom](https://remi-coulom.fr/Amsterdam2007)), and reliability-gated history both address cold history at the start of each search, and both shrink once history persists across moves. A reduction table fitted to the probability that a move becomes best overlaps Known history-based LMR. A policy head used only at depth 6 or more reuses the failed policy head behind a stop-gradient, where it can no longer hurt the value net. It must cut nodes to a fixed depth by more than 5% to earn a test.

### Architecture twists target data fragmentation, not width

| Idea (source field) | Verdict and nearest prior art | Expected Elo | When | First test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Phase-interpolated output experts, exported as hard buckets (tapered eval, soft mixtures of experts) | Partially known; proposed on TalkChess, never implemented | 0 to +8 | Now | 1 head vs 8 hard buckets vs 2-head tapered vs 8-knot interpolated, same data and seed |
| Per-bucket scale and shift before the SCReLU clamp (FiLM) | Novel | 0 to +8 | Now | Shift-only and scale-plus-shift over 8 buckets, vs baseline and output buckets |
| Cached pawn-king subnetwork feeding the output (Ethereal; hash-cached eval) | Partially known; Ethereal ran one beside a hand-crafted eval | +3 to +15 on a single layer | Now | Validation with and without; pawn-king hit rate in bench |
| Low-rank king-bucket residuals (LoRA plus factorisers) | Partially known; factorisers are Known | +3 to +10 over factorised buckets | 0.3-1B positions | No buckets, 8 factorised, rank 4, 16 and 64 |
| Nested (Matryoshka) accumulator with lazy tail updates | Novel as a combination | −5 to +10 | Hidden size ≥ 512 | Full-width validation unchanged under the nested loss |
| Block-sparse feature-transformer weights (2:4 sparsity) | Novel | −5 to +10 | When width is limited by speed | Dense h256 vs 50%-sparse h512 at equal update cost |
| King-bucket layout learned by clustering | Novel (method) | 0 to +5 | With king buckets | Hand-made vs clustered 8-bucket layout |

**ChessSI's eight hard output buckets probably failed from data fragmentation, and two novel twists address that.** Each bucket's 512 output weights saw about an eighth of 64.6M positions, and the buckets came out level (−0.7 ± 14.7). Phase-interpolated experts keep the phase specialisation while sharing data. Training blends neighbouring heads linearly over piece count, and export writes the interpolated values out as ordinary hard buckets at zero inference cost. A TalkChess post proposed interpolating phase networks, but no implementation was found ([TalkChess, snippet](https://talkchess.com/viewtopic.php?p=879993)). The second twist is a FiLM-style gain and offset per bucket, applied before the SCReLU clamp ([FiLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.07871)). It lets one feature detector saturate in middlegames and stay active in endgames, a phase-dependent nonlinearity that output buckets cannot express because they are linear in the activations. It costs 2H parameters per bucket and one multiply and one add per neuron at evaluation.

**Two more architecture ideas have real upside.** A cached pawn-king subnetwork, evaluated only when the pawn-king key changes, adds the multi-pawn interactions a single SCReLU layer represents poorly. Ethereal ran one beside its hand-crafted eval with over 92% cache hits ([TalkChess, snippet](https://talkchess.com/viewtopic.php?p=861680)), and feeding one into an NNUE was proposed but no result was ever reported ([TalkChess, snippet](https://talkchess.com/viewtopic.php?p=924777)). For the 0.3-1 billion stage, low-rank king-bucket residuals write each bucket's weights as W_b = W_shared + U_b·V and expand them into ordinary buckets at export. That combines LoRA ([arXiv 2106.09685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685)) with the Known factoriser, and turns the bucket count into a rank dial that can grow with the data. Every architecture change should be tested both at fixed nodes and at 10+0.1. Viridithas's 16 king buckets gained +31.64 at 25,000 nodes but only +4.83 at 40+0.4, and its threat inputs gained +27.43 at fixed nodes but only +2.98 at 8+0.08 ([viridithas-networks releases](https://github.com/cosmobobak/viridithas-networks/releases)).

The Kind column has only Known, Novel and Known + Novel. The cleanest fix is a fourth value, "Partially known", which the queue's prose already uses; it needs the owner's approval, because it changes the benchmark's categories.

**Several more entries keep their labels but need sharper notes.** The auxiliary head can stay Novel for NNUE, since no NNUE trainer has a training-only head. But its note says KataGo's and Lc0's heads are used at inference, when Lc0's categorical value head is in fact training-only and left out of the exported network. That makes it the nearest chess precedent and worth citing, even though its target is the same value split into buckets rather than a second, cleaner target. The uncertainty-aware evaluation stays Partially known, with its baseline sharpened from fixed margins to abs(correction) scaling. The self-filtered opening book stays Known in principle; its blinded, per-band information selection is the new part. The principled version of truncated-game scoring should be logged as a failed novel methodology once the retrospective is rerun on all games. The queue's "SCReLU multiply trick" can be struck, since `nnue.cpp` already does it.

## A three-queue plan for the main PC

**The plan runs three queues at once, because they spend different resources.** The CPU queue (Claude Code's cores 0-13) carries the SPRTs and is the bottleneck. The GPU queue screens training ideas on net9's frozen snapshot at no CPU cost. The datagen queue changes what the second PC records. Steps 1-4 cost no games and can start while step 5's SPRT runs; GPU steps 8-12 run alongside CPU steps 5-7; and datagen steps 15-16 run whenever the second PC is free. GPU screens that clear their gate join the CPU queue as NNUE SPRTs, one at a time.

## Conclusion

**ChessSI's best chance at genuine novelty lies where its economics differ from those of the engines it is measured against.** Most transplants from SAT solving, planning, MCTS and shogi turned out to be chess techniques under other names, and the architecture ideas that are truly new promise little at ChessSI's data scale. What remains open is the part of engine development that mainstream projects rarely publish and that ChessSI's situation makes cheap: how training labels are built from self-play, and how accept-or-reject decisions are drawn from games. ChessSI's GPU sits idle while its games are expensive. Offline methods that modern engines abandoned for online histories and SPSA therefore become rational again wherever they save games. The passed auxiliary head is the template for what wins in this space: a change that costs GPU minutes, comes with a mechanism and an ablation, and replicates.

**The testing ideas are not an efficiency tweak; they are what lets the benchmark see novel gains at all.** The novel candidates cluster in the range where a [0, 5] SPRT is slowest. Without cheaper decisions, the project will log most of its most interesting proposals as inconclusive, and its hit rate will measure CPU budget rather than research judgment. And because every Novel verdict here rests on sources that excluded TalkChess threads, fishtest's history and Discord, a short check of those channels before an idea is logged as Novel would protect the column the benchmark exists to fill.
