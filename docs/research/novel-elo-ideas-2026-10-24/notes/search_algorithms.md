# Search-algorithm ideas for ChessSI: transplants from other fields, learned search components, and the Known backlog

Status: complete, 2026-10-24. Researcher: Claude Opus 5.5 (research subagent).

**Engine baseline used to judge novelty and fit** (read from `src/search.cpp`, `src/tt.cpp`, `src/tt.h` and `docs/IDEAS.md` at `origin/claude/artifact-access-issue-ug96zp`): one 16-byte TT entry per slot (full key, move, score, depth, bound; no static eval), always-replace except that a fail-low store keeps the old move for the same key; TT cutoffs at non-PV nodes with `depth >= depth`; PVS; aspiration from depth 5 (±25, doubling); check extension only; qsearch captures-only with MVV-LVA and SEE<0 skip, no TT, no check evasions; two killers; one butterfly history `[stm][from][to]` with gravity, reset on every `go` (the `Searcher` is rebuilt per move); NMP R = 3 + d/6 when eval >= beta, fail-soft except mates; LMR from a log-log table, quiets only, after the third legal move, -1 at PV, no history term; LMP (3 + d², quiets only, d <= 8); futility (eval + 120d <= alpha, d <= 6); RFP (d <= 3, 150d, with a full move generation to rule out stalemate on each cutoff); no cut-node flag, no IIR, no singular/double extensions, no ProbCut, no razoring, no capture/continuation/pawn/correction history, no mate-distance pruning; time = available/25 + 3/4·inc, hard = 3x soft, no stability, node-share or score-trend logic.

**Method and limits.** Prior-art checks read the current sources of Stockfish (master effdc3f, 17 Oct 2026), Reckless (69aea90, 23 Oct 2026), PlentyChess (9ca0057, 21 Oct 2026) and YaneuraOu (bd6db90, 23 Oct 2026) from read-only shallow clones; searched commit histories of Stockfish, Alexandria, Sirius, Viridithas and Stormphrax with GitHub commit search; read Stash's changelog and KataGo's methods document; and ran web searches (2026-10-24). talkchess.com, lichess.org and arxiv.org could not be fetched from this container (DNS failures), so TalkChess and Lichess items below come from search-result snippets only and are marked as such. Engine-dev Discord servers, where much current work is discussed, could not be searched. "Novel" therefore means "not found in these sources", not "proven new".

## Q1. Which search heuristics from shogi, Go, SAT/CSP, planning and MCTS/bandits have no chess equivalent yet, and which could plausibly help an alpha-beta chess engine?

### Takeaway
Most transplant candidates already have a chess twin: SAT activity scores (VSIDS, CHB) are the history heuristic with gravity, clause learning maps to TT/killers/correction history, MCTS's RAVE and progressive widening map to history and LMP, KataGo's subtree value bias is correction history, shogi's realization-probability search is close to Giraffe's probability-limited search, and YaneuraOu's search is a Stockfish port. The mechanisms with no chess twin found are: keying search-learned corrections by the evaluator's own representation, a learned uncertainty output used inside alpha-beta, MIP-style reliability gating between cheap statistics and expensive probes, statistically calibrated and randomly audited pruning, and sequential halving at the root. Nine ranked candidates built on these (deliverable a) are at the end of the Inferences below.

### Cited Findings
**SAT / CSP / MIP**
- CHB (conflict-history-based branching, Liang, Ganesh, Poupart, Czarnecki, AAAI 2016) replaces VSIDS bumping with the exponential recency weighted average (ERWA) from bandits; CHB-based MiniSat solved about 16.1% more instances than its VSIDS version, Glucose about 5.6% more. — [AAAI 2016](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/10439)
- LRB (learning-rate branching) extends ERWA with reason-side rate and locality and was faster than VSIDS and CHB on 1,975 SAT-competition instances (2009-2014). — [LRB paper (PDF)](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~ppoupart/publications/sat/learning-rate-branching-heuristic-SAT.pdf)
- Conflict-History Search (CSP, 2019) uses ERWA to track how constraint hardness evolves during search. — [JFPC 2019 (PDF)](https://pageperso.lis-lab.fr/cyril.terrioux/en/publis/jfpc2019b.pdf)
- MIP reliability branching (Achterberg, Koch, Martin, Oper. Res. Letters 33, 2005) uses expensive strong branching for a candidate until its pseudocost history has enough observations (threshold η), then switches to cheap pseudocosts; SCIP's default hybrid branching builds on it. — [ZIB report "Branching rules revisited"](https://opus4.kobv.de/opus4-zib/frontdoor/index/index/docId/788); [SCIP 8.0](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.08872)
- The relative history heuristic (Winands, van der Werf, van den Herik, Uiterwijk, CG 2004) divides history scores by butterfly counts (how often each move was searched), cutting 10-15% of nodes in Lines of Action; the CPW notes the butterfly heuristic itself was never tested in a real chess program. — [Maastricht](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/the-relative-history-heuristic/); [CPW Relative History Heuristic](https://chessprogramming.org/Relative_History_Heuristic)

**Planning**
- Thayer, Dionne and Ruml (ICAPS 2011) learn corrections to a heuristic online, during search, from one-step errors (a running average of the error between a node and its best child), giving faster search and better solutions. — [ICAPS 2011](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/view/13474); [summary in EECBS, arXiv 2010.01367](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.01367)
- Width-based planning (IW(1)) prunes any state that makes no atom true for the first time; IW(1) and IW(2) solved 37% and 88% of 37,921 single-goal instances (secondary description). — [Width-based planning survey, arXiv 2106.04866](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.04866)

**MCTS / bandits / Go**
- Gumbel MuZero (Danihelka, Guez, Schrittwieser, Silver, ICLR 2022) runs Sequential Halving at the root to minimise simple regret; it matched the state of the art in Go, chess and Atari and improved substantially with few simulations. — [ICLR 2022](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2022/poster/6418); [mlanthology](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2022/danihelka2022iclr-policy)
- Coulom (ICGA J. 30(4), 2007) fit Bradley-Terry "Elo ratings" of move-pattern features from 652 games (34.9% prediction rate); a Monte-Carlo program using them reached the level of the strongest classical programs. — [Coulom, Amsterdam 2007](https://remi-coulom.fr/Amsterdam2007); [ICGA PDF](https://www.remi-coulom.fr/Amsterdam2007/icgaj.pdf)
- KataGo uncertainty-weighted playouts: auxiliary heads predict the squared error between the current short-term value/score prediction (stop-gradient) and an exponentially averaged future MCTS value (Huber loss); playouts are down-weighted roughly in proportion to predicted uncertainty, with a floor. Reported only together with dynamic variance-scaled cPUCT (cPUCT scaled by about the square root of the node's utility variance): about +75 Elo over the previous release, about +50 if the old cPUCT had been tuned. — [KataGo Methods](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/docs/KataGoMethods.md)
- KataGo subtree value bias correction: buckets keyed by the last mover, last and previous move locations, the 5x5 pattern around the last move (with atari info) and the ko point store a visit-weighted average of (NN utility minus subtree MCTS utility); new nodes use NN utility minus 0.35 x bucket bias; reported +30 to +60 Elo. KataGo's optimistic policy is reported at +40 to +90 Elo. — [KataGo Methods](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/docs/KataGoMethods.md)
- KataGo's short-term value targets exist to supply lower-variance training feedback and enable uncertainty-weighted playouts and optimistic policy. — [Wu 2019, arXiv 1902.10565](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.10565); [KataGo Methods](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/docs/KataGoMethods.md)
- A Lc0 fork (Ergodice/lc0) implements KataGo-style uncertainty weighting using winrate error only, off by default, and reports +20 Elo at LTC with BT3 networks (fork; unverified). — [Ergodice/lc0](https://github.com/Ergodice/lc0)

**Shogi and probability-driven depth**
- Realization probability search (Tsuruoka, Yokoyama, Chikayama; shogi) gives each node the product of the transition probabilities of the moves leading to it (probabilities per move category, learned from professional game records) and makes it a leaf when that falls below a threshold; Enhanced RPS beat RPS 62.5% over 600 games in Lines of Action; a later shogi paper updates the probabilities from search results. No chess test of RPS was found. — [Winands & Björnsson, Enhanced RPS (PDF)](https://staff.ru.is/yngvi/pdf/WinandsB08a.pdf); [ICGA Journal](https://content.iospress.com/articles/icga-journal/icg25304); [RPS based on search results (CiNii)](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1050282677602545024)
- Giraffe (Lai, 2015) used a move-evaluator network estimating the probability that each legal move is best (best move in its top 3 70% of the time among about 35 legal moves) to drive a probability-based search, and reported a significant increase in playing strength from it. — [Giraffe, arXiv 1509.01549](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01549); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1509.01549)
- YaneuraOu's search (master bd6db90) is a close port of Stockfish, including correction history; its own additions (comments marked "やねうら王独自") are a mate-in-1 check (`Mate::mate_1ply`) at non-root nodes without a TT hit, stopping iterative deepening once depth exceeds about 2.5x the mate distance found, ponder-move fallbacks and output throttling. — [YaneuraOu yaneuraou-search.cpp @ bd6db90](https://github.com/yaneurao/YaneuraOu/blob/bd6db90a7ac91875356f6e4ac61a18c9031a9116/source/engine/yaneuraou-engine/yaneuraou-search.cpp)

**Hybrids and chess context**
- Scorpio supports Huang-style alpha-beta rollouts with LMR and null move in the tree part, combined with MCTS rollouts or standard alpha-beta; ScorpioNN 2.9 evaluates with a neural network through egbbdll. — [Scorpio repository](https://github.com/dshawul/Scorpio/); [CPW Scorpio](https://chessprogramming.org/Scorpio)
- Stoofvlees II, the 2023 WCCC winner, is a hybrid MCTS engine with a deep convolutional network and a classical evaluator, not an alpha-beta engine. — [ICGA Journal 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/13896911251360654); [CPW Stoofvlees](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Stoofvlees)
- ProbCut and Multi-ProbCut cut when a shallow search predicts, via a linear regression with residual σ, that the deep search will exceed beta; MPC was effective in Othello and shogi, plain ProbCut did not help Crafty, and MPC scored 59.4% in a 64-game chess match. — [Jiang & Buro 2003 (PDF)](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~jiang/papers/mpc_main.pdf); [CPW ProbCut](https://chessprogramming.org/ProbCut); [AAAI 2006 paper citing MPC in chess](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/%7Eleews/publications/AAAI06-161.pdf)

**Ranking criteria.** Expected Elo per CPU hour, weighted toward ideas whose novelty verdict is strongest, because SPRT CPU is ChessSI's bottleneck and novel results are the benchmark signal. Every candidate below has an offline gate (no games) so dead ideas die for CPU minutes, not SPRT hours. Game counts assume SPRT [0, 5] with logistic-Elo bounds and per-game score variance 0.14-0.18 (Brownian approximation, my calculation): true +20 → about 2,300-2,900 games; +10 → 5,300-6,800; +5 or 0 → 14,000-18,000; +2.5 → 23,000-30,000. At about 1,700 games/hour on cores 0-13 (the brief's 2,500/hour on the full PC scaled to 13 of 19 concurrent games), a +5 idea or a null idea costs roughly 8-11 hours. This matches ChessSI's own record (history heuristic at −2.7 took 11,690 games; SEE pruning at +6 reached LLR 2.48 after 9,398). Per `docs/IDEAS.md`, pruning-margin ideas should go to 10+0.1 even when the 2+0.02 pre-screen is near zero, because pruning gains grow with depth.

Testing them on the bare engine would credit them with gains the Known technique would have given anyway.

**1. Representation-keyed correction history ("NNUE-space correction")**
- *Idea.* Keep correction-history tables keyed not only by pawn or piece-subset hashes but also by a short binary signature of the network's own accumulator: the signs (above/below a per-unit median) of K ≈ 12-20 chosen hidden units of the side-to-move accumulator, hashed into a 16-64K-entry table and updated with the same rule as a pawn correction table. Positions the network represents alike share a correction, so a search-discovered bias generalises along the network's own notion of similarity. Sources: KataGo's subtree value bias keyed by a local pattern ([KataGo Methods](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/docs/KataGoMethods.md)), online heuristic-error learning in planning ([Thayer et al.](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/view/13474)), sign-bit hashing (locality-sensitive hashing).
- *Prior art: Novel (not found).* Correction keys found: Stockfish pawn, minor-piece, per-colour non-pawn and continuation (2 and 4 plies) ([search.cpp](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/effdc3fd3f6de6e75fe1374a51a7a7eed66f880b/src/search.cpp)), with a material key added in 2024 ([ddc9f48](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/commit/ddc9f48bc3e0b1d22b2d1259d5d45d4640e0374d)) and major/minor/non-pawn keys from Sirius and Starzix ([60351b9](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/commit/60351b9df901ff5278f208a9cf3a40059ff54832)); Reckless pawn, per-colour non-pawn and continuation keys, each split into 16 fifty-move-clock buckets ([search.rs](https://github.com/codedeliveryservice/Reckless/blob/69aea9086899189ee38fd3e556bf9469b805552a/src/search.rs), [board.rs](https://github.com/codedeliveryservice/Reckless/blob/69aea9086899189ee38fd3e556bf9469b805552a/src/board.rs)); PlentyChess pawn, non-pawn, minor, major and continuation ([history.h](https://github.com/Yoshie2000/PlentyChess/blob/9ca005740a35967c1403bfb9a3a9032c97ee003d/src/history.h)); Sirius pawn, material, non-pawn, minor+king, major+king ([commits](https://github.com/mcthouacbb/Sirius/commit/97b85bbaac88ff5a0f63e28776027dd3de77164e)); Viridithas continuation correction (2025-09) ([9082e54](https://github.com/cosmobobak/viridithas/commit/9082e54dcf1143d4ee2a12ca7ed9f69ee7046342)). A GitHub code search for accumulator-keyed correction returned nothing relevant; Discord was not searchable.
- *Why for ChessSI.* Hand-picked keys capture errors tied to pawn structure or piece sets; NNUE errors also come from features the net encodes in its own way (king exposure, piece coordination). The signature costs a few SIMD compares on an accumulator already computed for the static eval. ChessSI's small 256-unit net and its own-data training make its errors systematic and its unit statistics easy to log.
- *Expected Elo.* +0 to +6 on top of a pawn + non-pawn + continuation correction history, low confidence. Reasoning: Stockfish's three extra keys together gave about +4.4 (STC) and +8.7 (LTC) Elo computed from the W/L/D in [60351b9](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/commit/60351b9df901ff5278f208a9cf3a40059ff54832); a key with different error structure could add a few Elo, or nothing if activations mostly re-encode material and pawns.
- *Cost.* Development about a day after the Known correction history; offline test minutes of CPU; one SPRT (about 14-18k games, 8-11 h, if the true gain is ~+5).
- *Experiment.* (a) A logging build records, at nodes with depth >= 2 and not in check, the signs of 32 candidate units, the corrected static eval, the search value, bound and depth, over bench plus a few hundred self-play games. (b) Offline, replay the nodes in search order and simulate the table-update rule for: pawn + non-pawn keys (baseline), baseline + accumulator key with K = 12/16/20 (units selected on one log file by greedy residual-variance reduction, tested on another), and baseline + a random-bit control key of the same width. (c) Continue only if the accumulator key reduces mean squared residual clearly more than the random control; then implement and SPRT [0, 5]. All data are ChessSI's own searches.
- *Risks.* Redundancy with existing keys; table thrashing if K is large; unit selection is per network (store the chosen indices with the net); selection overfitting; a correlated-tree artefact (any key helps a little), which the random control is there to expose.

**2. Log-calibrated pruning margins (ProbCut generalised to static-eval pruning)**
- *Idea.* Replace the hand-set margins (RFP 150·d to depth 3, futility 120·d to depth 6, NMP gate eval >= beta) by margins read from the measured distribution of (depth-d search value − corrected static eval) in ChessSI's own searches, conditioned on cheap features (depth, material/phase bucket, abs(correction) once it exists, improving, side has a threatened piece). Prune when the fitted probability that the full search would have changed the bound is below ε. Sources: ProbCut/MPC ([Jiang & Buro](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~jiang/papers/mpc_main.pdf)), learned heuristic-error models in planning ([Thayer et al.](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/view/13474)), quantile calibration.
- *Prior art: Partially known.* ProbCut/MPC use a regression of deep on shallow search with σ-scaled thresholds (MPC 59.4% in 64 chess games) ([CPW ProbCut](https://chessprogramming.org/ProbCut)); Stockfish and Reckless widen margins by abs(correction) ([Stockfish](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/effdc3fd3f6de6e75fe1374a51a7a7eed66f880b/src/search.cpp), [Reckless](https://github.com/codedeliveryservice/Reckless/blob/69aea9086899189ee38fd3e556bf9469b805552a/src/search.rs)); margins elsewhere are hand-set and SPSA-tuned (e.g. Alexandria's "extract the hyperparameters ... tuned version" +21.14 ± 9.13, [9f81a27](https://github.com/PGG106/Alexandria/commit/9f81a272d936811710e19d92bc1f785e3e5a5d5f)). Not found: static-eval pruning margins set from per-bucket conditional quantiles of the engine's own residuals.
- *Why for ChessSI.* Its margins were chosen before the NNUE changed the eval scale; RFP stops at depth 3, while Stockfish's equivalent runs to depth < 19 outside mate searches ([search.cpp](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/effdc3fd3f6de6e75fe1374a51a7a7eed66f880b/src/search.cpp)); and SPSA needs tens of thousands of games while fitting from logs costs CPU minutes.
- *Expected Elo.* +5 to +20 against the current margins (moderate confidence), but much of that is what Known re-tuning would also give; the increment over an SPSA-tuned version is unknown, perhaps 0 to +5. Novelty credit applies only to the calibration method; log it as "Known + Novel".
- *Cost.* 1-2 days (logging build, fitting script, margin table); 1-2 CPU-hours of logging; SPRT about 3-7k games if the gain is +10 to +15.
- *Experiment.* (a) Logging build: at a random 1/16 of non-PV, not-in-check nodes with depth 1-8, search fully even when RFP/futility would prune and record (d, eval, alpha, beta, value, bound, features), on positions from ChessSI's own games. (b) Fit P(value >= beta | eval − beta, d, bucket) (logistic or empirical quantiles); margin(d, bucket) = the (1−ε) quantile, ε = 1% (then 0.5% and 2%). (c) SPRT vs current margins; (d) separately, extend RFP to depth 6-8 with the calibrated margins. Candidate 4's audits can supply the unbiased labels.
- *Risks.* Selection bias (the other prunings stay on); fat tactical tails need small ε; sparse buckets at high depth; margins must be refit per network.

**3. Learned uncertainty output for pruning, reductions, aspiration and time**
- *Idea.* Add a second NNUE output, trained with stop-gradient into the accumulator, that predicts the size of the static eval's error (abs(search score − static eval), in win-probability units), as KataGo's auxiliary heads predict short-term value error. Use u(pos) to widen RFP/futility margins, to reduce LMR less, to widen the aspiration window, and at the root only to scale the soft time limit.
- *Prior art: Partially known.* KataGo uses learned uncertainty to weight MCTS playouts ([KataGo Methods](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/docs/KataGoMethods.md)); an Lc0 fork reports +20 Elo at LTC ([Ergodice/lc0](https://github.com/Ergodice/lc0), unverified); alpha-beta engines use abs(correction) as an unlearned proxy in the same places (Stockfish RFP, singular margins and LMR; Reckless RFP, futility, singular, LMR and qsearch SEE). CPW's "Uncertainty Cut-Offs" (Turk) is an unrelated cut-node heuristic ([CPW](https://chessprogramming.org/Uncertainty_Cut-Offs)). A web search for an NNUE uncertainty/variance head in alpha-beta found nothing (2026-10-24).
- *Why for ChessSI.* The trainer already supports extra heads (training-only auxiliary heads passed at +53.2 ± 15.6 and +34.6 ± 12.2 per `docs/IDEAS.md`); a stop-gradient head cannot hurt the value net, unlike the policy head that raised validation loss; and abs(correction) only reflects errors the search has already seen in similar positions, while a head predicts error before search.
- *Expected Elo.* +0 to +10 over abs(corr)-scaled margins, low confidence; larger if correction history is absent, but then it competes with a Known gain.
- *Cost.* GPU hours for training; 1-2 days to export and use a second output; inference about one extra 512-wide dot product per node, or less if computed lazily only when a margin decision is borderline; SPRT 5-18k games.
- *Experiment.* (a) Datagen also records the static eval (already needed for the queued "train on positions the eval gets wrong" idea). (b) Train the head on net8's data with a frozen trunk; on held-out games, measure Spearman correlation between u and the actual error, then the partial correlation after controlling for abs(corr) (from candidate 1's logs) and phase. Stop if the partial correlation is near zero. (c) First use: margins += k·u with k fitted from logs as in candidate 2; SPRT. (d) Root-only time scaling as a separate SPRT.
- *Risks.* NPS cost; the head may only learn phase or material; noisy targets; overlap with correction history.

**4. Randomised pruning audits (measurement first, then online calibration)**
- *Idea.* With small probability p (e.g. 1/256), a node that RFP, futility, LMP or NMP would prune is searched anyway (a shadow search) and the outcome logged: would the full search have changed the bound? Phase 1 only measures false-prune rates by technique, depth and bucket in real games; phase 2 adjusts per-depth margin multipliers online toward a target error rate, carried across the game.
- *Prior art: Novel (not found).* Verified null-move pruning re-searches every null-move cutoff at reduced depth to catch zugzwang ([Tabibi & Netanyahu, arXiv 0808.1125](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/0808.1125)); engine authors report how often each pruning fires, e.g. Arasan futility 34.70% of search pruning (TalkChess "Verification of pruning techniques", [snippet only](https://talkchess.com/viewtopic.php?p=643980)); Smith and Nau modelled when forward pruning works ([TR98-07](https://webdocs.cs.ualberta.ca/~tony/TechnicalReports/TR98-07.pdf)). No random-sample verification or error-rate controller found.
- *Why for ChessSI.* It gives the benchmark measured pruning error rates (research data in their own right), removes candidate 2's selection bias, and lets margins adapt to the time control.
- *Expected Elo.* Phase 1: 0 (off in play). Phase 2: −3 to +5, low confidence.
- *Cost.* About a day; 1-3% extra nodes at p = 1/256; phase 2 SPRT 14-18k games.
- *Experiment.* Audits behind a UCI option; 1,000 self-play games at 10+0.1 with logging (no SPRT); report false-prune rates per technique and depth; feed them to candidate 2; then SPRT the controller against the calibrated static margins.
- *Risks.* Controller noise; an arbitrary target ε; shadow searches must not update history, killers or correction tables, or they bias them.

**5. Learned iterative-deepening stop rule (and a root moves-left estimate)**
- *Idea.* After each completed iteration, a small logistic model fitted on ChessSI's own searches predicts the probability that a much longer search would pick a different move, from best-move changes, score drop over recent iterations, aspiration failures, the best move's share of nodes, depth, abs(score) and phase. Stop early when it is low; extend toward the hard limit when high. Second part: replace the fixed movestogo = 25 by a root-only moves-left prediction (Lc0-style head, used only at the root, so free in NPS).
- *Prior art: Partially known.* Kocsis, Uiterwijk and van den Herik learned the per-move depth choice under time constraints with TD learning and genetic algorithms in Lines of Action (CG 2000) ([Maastricht](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/learning-time-allocation-using-neural-networks)); Giraffe listed neural time allocation as future work, noting engines use simple heuristics ([Giraffe](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01549), via search snippet); Burduli and Wu wrote "Time Management in a Chess Game through Machine Learning" ([Temple PDF](https://cis.temple.edu/~wu/research/publications/Publication_files/Paper_Guga.pdf), contents not verified); Stockfish multiplies its optimum time by hand-tuned falling-eval, best-move-instability and best-move-effort factors ([search.cpp](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/effdc3fd3f6de6e75fe1374a51a7a7eed66f880b/src/search.cpp)). Not found: a stop rule fitted to an alpha-beta engine's own search logs.
- *Why for ChessSI.* Its time management is the crudest part of the engine, and the Known version alone is worth +15 to +31 elsewhere (Q4); a model fitted to its own logs could replace hand-tuning of several factors at once.
- *Expected Elo.* Do Known TM first; the learned increment over it is perhaps 0 to +8, low confidence.
- *Cost.* 1-2 days; labels need a few CPU-hours (e.g. 20k positions re-searched for 4x the time used); SPRT 5-18k games.
- *Experiment.* Log iteration features during self-play at 10+0.1; label each by re-searching the root for 4x the time and recording whether the move changes; fit logistic regression (at most about ten features); offline AUC on held-out games; SPRT against Known TM (node share + stability).
- *Risks.* Labels depend on ChessSI's own longer search; time-control specificity; overfitting the thresholds.

**6. Reliability-gated history (MIP reliability-branching transplant)**
- *Idea.* Count updates per history entry (a saturating byte). For quiet moves whose entry has fewer than η observations, order them by a prior (candidate 7, or a PSQT delta from the PeSTO tables still in the code) instead of the noisy value, and skip history-based LMR adjustments for them. At rare high-depth nodes, optionally probe the top unreliable quiets with a qsearch-depth null-window search, the analog of strong branching.
- *Prior art: Partially known.* The relative history heuristic normalises by counts but does not gate ([CPW RHH](https://chessprogramming.org/Relative_History_Heuristic)); MIP reliability branching gates on η ([ZIB](https://opus4.kobv.de/opus4-zib/frontdoor/index/index/docId/788)); IID is the known expensive probe for the TT move only. Not found in chess.
- *Why for ChessSI.* History is reset on every `go`, so most entries are cold early in each search; gravity conflates "never seen" with "neutral".
- *Expected Elo.* 0 to +5, low confidence; shrinks once history persists across moves.
- *Cost.* Under a day; one byte per entry; SPRT 14-30k games.
- *Experiment.* After the Known history-persistence change, test η in {2, 4} with the PSQT prior; gate on first-move-cutoff rate and nodes-to-depth on bench before the SPRT.
- *Risks.* Tiny effect; counts are mostly high once history persists.

**7. Bradley-Terry move-feature prior learned from ChessSI's own search logs**
- *Idea.* Fit Bradley-Terry ratings of quiet-move features (piece, from/to region, gives check, SEE sign, moves out of attack, moves into attack, pawn-push rank, phase) from "which move was best/cut at this node" events in ChessSI's searches; use the summed ratings as a static prior that seeds history at each `go` and breaks ties, decaying as history learns (MCTS progressive bias). This generalises the queued "history prior learned offline".
- *Prior art: Partially known.* Learned move ordering: Kocsis et al., 20-50% faster than the history heuristic in Lines of Action, and a chess follow-up, the Neural MoveMap heuristic ([Maastricht](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/move-ordering-using-neural-networks), [MoveMap in chess](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/the-neural-movemap-heuristic-in-chess/)); Giraffe's move evaluator ([Giraffe](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01549)); NNOM (2022), an NNUE-inspired 98304x512x384 move-ordering network with no Elo reported (TalkChess, [snippet only](https://talkchess.com/viewtopic.php?p=923514)); Bradley-Terry patterns in Go ([Coulom](https://remi-coulom.fr/Amsterdam2007)). Not found: Bradley-Terry feature ratings as a cold-start prior in a modern NNUE alpha-beta engine.
- *Why for ChessSI.* Cold-start ordering after each history reset; no countermove, continuation or capture history yet; a few table lookups per move.
- *Expected Elo.* 0 to +8 now, 0 to +3 once history persists; low confidence.
- *Cost.* About a day; minutes of logging and fitting; SPRT 10-18k games.
- *Experiment.* Log the legal quiet moves and the cutoff/best move at nodes with depth >= 2; fit Bradley-Terry by minorisation-maximisation or logistic regression on a held-out split; gate on bench cutoff rate; SPRT.
- *Risks.* Duplicates history; labels biased by the current ordering (moves tried first become best more often), which logging at sampled nodes with shuffled quiet order would reduce.

**8. Probability-driven reduction table (RPS / Giraffe transplant)**
- *Idea.* Replace the log-log LMR table by r = round(a·(−ln p) − b), where p is the fitted probability that the move becomes best given move index, history bucket, killer/check/capture flags and depth, estimated from ChessSI's own logs; choose a and b so the mean reduction matches today's, isolating the reshaping effect.
- *Prior art: Partially known, close.* RPS in shogi and Lines of Action (above); Giraffe's probability-based search with a learned move evaluator; a TalkChess thread ("On LMR, and statically predicting moves") argues that reductions should follow move probabilities estimated from hash/capture/killer/history signals ([snippet only](https://talkchess.com/viewtopic.php?p=637922)); modern engines approximate this online with history-based LMR (Q4).
- *Why for ChessSI.* Its LMR ignores history and move type; one fitted model captures both.
- *Expected Elo.* 0 to +10, low confidence, largely overlapping Known history-based LMR.
- *Cost.* 1-2 days; minutes of logging; SPRT 5-18k games.
- *Experiment.* Log per searched move features and outcome (raised alpha / best / cutoff) with LMR disabled at sampled nodes (unbiased labels); fit logistic regression; check predicted vs actual cutoff rate by move index; SPRT against current LMR plus the Known history term.
- *Risks.* May reduce to Known history-LMR; ordering-biased labels; per-move cost.

**9. Frozen-trunk policy head used only at high-depth nodes**
- *Idea.* Train a move-scoring head (128 from/to logits) on ChessSI's own search best moves, reading the existing accumulator with stop-gradient so it cannot degrade the value net; evaluate it only at nodes with depth >= 6, a small fraction of nodes, to order quiets after the TT move, good captures and killers.
- *Prior art: Partially known.* Learned ordering (Kocsis; Giraffe; NNOM, above); a Lichess bot "NNUEandTheVoices" is described as Reckless alpha-beta with Leela's policy ordering moves near the root ([profile, snippet only](https://lichess.org/@/NNUEandTheVoices), unverified, and a Leela policy would break ChessSI's own-data rule); Scorpio's alpha-beta-rollout hybrids. Not found: a stop-gradient policy head on an NNUE accumulator, depth-gated, inside alpha-beta.
- *Why for ChessSI.* Ordering quality at high-depth nodes drives LMR/LMP, and ChessSI's quiet ordering is weak there. ChessSI's own policy auxiliary head hurt validation loss when trained jointly (`docs/IDEAS.md`); stop-gradient removes that conflict.
- *Expected Elo.* −5 to +15, low confidence; NPS cost and the Known histories may erase it.
- *Cost.* A few GPU-hours; 2-3 days; about 64k multiply-adds per gated node; SPRT 5-18k games.
- *Experiment.* Offline top-1/top-3 accuracy against history ordering at logged depth >= 6 nodes; nodes to a fixed depth on bench; SPRT only if nodes drop by more than 5% at equal depth.
- *Risks.* Cost; overlap with Known histories; distribution shift between data generations.

**Considered and not recommended**
- *Sequential halving at the root (Gumbel MuZero).* Searching the bottom half of root moves one ply shallower each round; novel for alpha-beta as far as found, but PVS null windows and root LMR already make weak root moves cheap, and root misses are the most costly. Expected −10 to +3.
- *Novelty pruning (IW).* Chess evaluations are dense and tactics repeat features, so pruning states without a "new atom" has no mechanism for Elo.
- *CHB/LRB step-size schedules for history.* A one-line experiment (larger gravity step early in each search), but expected about 0.
- *SAT restarts, YaneuraOu mate-in-1 at nodes, KataGo optimistic policy.* Already covered by iterative deepening and qsearch/check extension, or require a policy network ChessSI does not use.

### Gaps
- Discord engine-development servers (where many 2025-2026 search ideas first appear) and TalkChess pages could not be read, so novelty verdicts for candidates 1, 3 and 4 rest on source reading of five engines, GitHub searches and web searches.
- No Elo figure was found for RPS in chess, for Bradley-Terry ordering in chess, or for policy-in-alpha-beta chess hybrids; the NNOM thread and the Lichess hybrid bot are known only from search snippets.

## Q2. Which learned search components trained offline on an engine's own search data have been tried in chess, how did they do, and what remains novel?

### Takeaway
Offline-learned search control is old in chess research but has been abandoned by modern engines: learned move ordering (Kocsis et al. 2001; Giraffe 2015; NNOM 2022), probability-driven depth (Giraffe), learned extension weights (Björnsson and Marsland 2003), statistically fitted cut thresholds (ProbCut/MPC) and learned time allocation (Kocsis et al. 2000, in Lines of Action). Top engines today learn search statistics online (history, continuation, correction tables) and tune constants with SPSA. No published learned LMR model, learned uncertainty output used in alpha-beta, or learned best-move-change time manager for a chess engine was found, so those remain open; the policy-in-alpha-beta space is occupied only by hybrids.

### Cited Findings
- Kocsis, Uiterwijk, Postma and van den Herik trained neural networks to rank moves and reported 20-50% search speed-ups over the history heuristic in Lines of Action; the chess follow-up, the Neural MoveMap heuristic, combined network and history scores. — [Move Ordering using Neural Networks](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/move-ordering-using-neural-networks); [Neural MoveMap heuristic in chess](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/the-neural-movemap-heuristic-in-chess/)
- Kocsis, Uiterwijk and van den Herik (CG 2000) used TD learning and genetic algorithms for the per-move depth choice under the remaining time in Lines of Action, with good results against existing time-allocation techniques; Kocsis's 2003 thesis "Learning Search Decisions" collects this line. — [Learning time allocation using neural networks](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/en/publications/learning-time-allocation-using-neural-networks)
- Giraffe's move evaluator estimated the probability that each move is best (best move in its top 3 70% of the time) and drove a probability-based search, reported as a significant strength gain; Giraffe also searched about 10x fewer positions per second than a conventional engine. — [Giraffe](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01549); [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2015/09/14/247956/deep-learning-machine-teaches-itself-chess-in-72-hours-plays-at-international-master/amp/)
- Björnsson and Marsland (Information Sciences 154, 2003) tuned fractional extension values for check, passed-pawn, recapture and one-reply extensions by gradient descent on the node count needed to solve test positions (described via secondary sources). — [Optimizing Selective Search in Chess, arXiv 1009.0550 (secondary description)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1009.0550); [CPW Yngvi Björnsson](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Yngvi_Björnsson)
- Multi-ProbCut (statistically fitted thresholds) scored 59.4% in 64 chess games after ProbCut had failed to help Crafty; a 2006 AAAI paper says MPC works in chess but needs a fair amount of work. — [Jiang & Buro (PDF)](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~jiang/papers/mpc_main.pdf); [AAAI 2006](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/%7Eleews/publications/AAAI06-161.pdf)
- NNOM (TalkChess, 2022, Levi Gibson): an NNUE-inspired 98304x512x384 move-ordering network computing only legal-move outputs, C inference and TensorFlow training; no NPS or Elo result seen. — [TalkChess thread (snippet only)](https://talkchess.com/viewtopic.php?p=923514)
- A web search for learned or neural late-move reductions found no engine or paper; Seer, an NNUE engine, lists LMR as a hand-coded feature. — [Seer README](https://github.com/connormcmonigle/seer-nnue)
- A TalkChess thread notes that most engines use a fixed reduction schedule by move number, that the ordering alone hides the move probabilities, and proposes estimating them from hash/capture/killer/history signals for both ordering and reduction; one engine there used 0.3·e^(0.112·move number). — [TalkChess "On LMR, and statically predicting moves" (snippet only)](https://talkchess.com/viewtopic.php?p=637922)
- Engines that do use learned depth or policy inside a tree search are hybrids: Scorpio's alpha-beta rollouts with MCTS, and Stoofvlees II (MCTS with a CNN and a classical evaluator). — [Scorpio](https://github.com/dshawul/Scorpio/); [Stoofvlees II, ICGA J. 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/13896911251360654)
- KataGo's learned uncertainty is used inside search (MCTS), and an Lc0 fork reports +20 Elo at LTC for the same idea (unverified). — [KataGo Methods](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/docs/KataGoMethods.md); [Ergodice/lc0](https://github.com/Ergodice/lc0)
- Learned time management for chess remains mostly a proposal: Giraffe lists neural time allocation as future work; Burduli and Wu wrote on ML time management in chess; the CPW lists heuristic best-move-stability rules. — [Giraffe](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.01549); [Burduli & Wu (PDF)](https://cis.temple.edu/~wu/research/publications/Publication_files/Paper_Guga.pdf); [CPW Time Management](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Time_Management)
- ChessSI's own record: a training-only auxiliary value head passed twice (+53.2 ± 15.6; +34.6 ± 12.2), while a jointly trained policy head raised validation loss at every weight tried; the queue already proposes an offline-learned history prior and an uncertainty-aware evaluation. — ChessSI `docs/IDEAS.md` at `origin/claude/artifact-access-issue-ug96zp` (local source)

### Inferences
- What remains novel for chess alpha-beta, as far as found: (1) a learned uncertainty output used for alpha-beta margins/LMR/time (candidate 3); (2) reductions or pruning thresholds fitted to the engine's own logged search outcomes rather than to SPSA game results (candidates 2 and 8); (3) a stop rule fitted to the engine's own iteration logs (candidate 5); (4) a stop-gradient policy head on an NNUE accumulator, used only at high-depth nodes (candidate 9).
- Why modern engines abandoned offline learning is instructive: online histories adapt within the search to the actual position, and SPSA optimises the real objective (game results). Offline-from-logs methods win only when they are much cheaper per decision, which is exactly ChessSI's situation: CPU for games is the bottleneck, while logging and fitting cost CPU minutes and GPU time is spare. The right claim for these ideas is "same Elo for far fewer games", tested by SPRT.
- Labels from the engine's own searches are biased by its current ordering and pruning; sampling a small fraction of nodes with the relevant pruning switched off (or audited, candidate 4) gives unbiased labels at negligible cost. This is a design rule for every learned component above.
- All proposed data sources (bench positions, self-play and SPRT games, re-searches by ChessSI itself) satisfy the own-data-only rule; none needs another engine's evaluations.

### Gaps
- No Elo measurement was found for Giraffe's probability-based search against a depth-based search with LMR, nor for NNOM.
- The full texts of Kocsis et al. (time allocation) and Björnsson and Marsland were not read; details come from abstracts and secondary sources.
- The Lc0 fork's +20 Elo uncertainty-weighting result is from the fork's own README and unverified.

## Q3. Can uncertainty or eval-error statistics set pruning margins adaptively, and how does that relate to Stockfish's correction history?

### Takeaway
Yes, and the simplest form is already Known: Stockfish and Reckless use the magnitude of the correction-history value as an uncertainty signal that widens RFP/futility margins, adjusts singular-extension margins and reduces LMR, and Stockfish sizes its aspiration window from the root move's mean squared score. ProbCut's σ-scaled thresholds are the classic statistical version. Not found, and so open for ChessSI: a learned uncertainty output (KataGo-style) inside alpha-beta, margins fitted from per-bucket residual quantiles of the engine's own searches, and random audits that measure and control false-prune rates.

### Cited Findings
- Stockfish master (effdc3f, 17 Oct 2026) adds abs(correctionValue)/198435 to its reverse-futility ("futility pruning: child node") margin, subtracts abs(correctionValue)/198368 from the double- and triple-extension margins of singular extensions, and reduces LMR by abs(correctionValue)/26310. — [Stockfish search.cpp @ effdc3f](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/effdc3fd3f6de6e75fe1374a51a7a7eed66f880b/src/search.cpp)
- Stockfish's correction value sums pawn, minor-piece, per-colour non-pawn and continuation (2 and 4 plies) correction histories. — [Stockfish search.cpp @ effdc3f](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/effdc3fd3f6de6e75fe1374a51a7a7eed66f880b/src/search.cpp); [CPW Static Evaluation Correction History](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Static_Evaluation_Correction_History)
- Stockfish's aspiration delta is 5 + threadIdx % 8 + abs(meanSquaredScore)/10193, i.e. sized by the root move's score volatility. — [Stockfish search.cpp @ effdc3f](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/effdc3fd3f6de6e75fe1374a51a7a7eed66f880b/src/search.cpp)
- Reckless (69aea90, 23 Oct 2026) widens RFP by 669·abs(corr)/1024, raises the futility value by 555·abs(corr)/1024, uses abs(corr) in singular margins, reduces LMR by 3417·abs(corr)/1024 (and 2255·abs(corr)/1024 at a second site), and loosens qsearch SEE pruning by min(abs(corr), 68). — [Reckless search.rs @ 69aea90](https://github.com/codedeliveryservice/Reckless/blob/69aea9086899189ee38fd3e556bf9469b805552a/src/search.rs)
- Correction history entered Stockfish on 2023-12-31 ("Idea from Caissa"): a pawn-keyed running average of (search result − static eval), skipping fail-highs by captures, with depth-scaled updates. — [Stockfish commit b4d995d](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/commit/b4d995d0d910044cf4ea2ad3ee30fd1d21070cd8)
- A Stockfish patch updating correction history after successful null-move pruning (quarter-size update) passed in May 2024 and was removed in June 2024 as a non-regression simplification. — [Stockfish 0c79736](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/commit/0c797367a3a9783ff87422d543eb2106fea3e948); [Stockfish 4151c06](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/commit/4151c06b744a3145617200ca8f76285aae193dc2)
- ProbCut/MPC set cut thresholds from the regression residual σ of deep on shallow search values. — [Jiang & Buro (PDF)](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~jiang/papers/mpc_main.pdf)
- KataGo learns to predict its own short-term value error and down-weights uncertain playouts; it also scales exploration by the observed utility variance. — [KataGo Methods](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/docs/KataGoMethods.md)
- In planning, Thayer, Dionne and Ruml correct heuristics online from observed one-step errors. — [ICAPS 2011](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/view/13474)
- Verified null-move pruning re-searches null-move cutoffs to catch zugzwang; no source was found that samples pruned nodes to estimate false-prune rates. — [arXiv 0808.1125](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/0808.1125); [TalkChess "Verification of pruning techniques" (snippet only)](https://talkchess.com/viewtopic.php?p=643980)
- A web search for a learned NNUE uncertainty or variance head used for pruning found nothing; the closest hit was CPW's "Uncertainty Cut-Offs", a 1990s cut-node technique from the Turk engine. — [CPW Uncertainty Cut-Offs](https://chessprogramming.org/Uncertainty_Cut-Offs)

### Inferences
- **Relation to correction history.** Correction history is a signed, search-derived, hash-keyed estimate of the static eval's bias in similar positions; its magnitude is used as an uncertainty proxy. It only knows about errors the search has already uncovered in positions sharing a key. A learned uncertainty head (candidate 3) estimates the size of the error before any search; a residual-quantile calibration (candidate 2) estimates the whole error distribution per bucket; audits (candidate 4) measure the consequence (wrong prunes) directly. They are complementary layers, and only the first is Known.
- **Labelling what is Known.** Known: abs(correction) in RFP/futility/singular/LMR/qsearch-SEE margins (Stockfish, Reckless); aspiration windows sized by score volatility (Stockfish); ProbCut σ thresholds; uncertainty-weighted MCTS (KataGo, Lc0 fork). Not found: learned NNUE uncertainty used in alpha-beta; per-bucket residual-quantile margins for static-eval pruning; random pruning audits; correction tables keyed by the network's activations.
- **Recommended order for ChessSI.** (1) Known: pawn + non-pawn + continuation correction history, then abs(corr) in the RFP/futility margins and LMR (one change at a time). (2) Candidate 4 phase 1 to measure false-prune rates. (3) Candidate 2 calibrated margins, extending RFP beyond depth 3. (4) Candidate 3 only if its offline partial correlation beyond abs(corr) is clearly positive. (5) Candidate 1 as a separate correction key.
- The queued "uncertainty-aware evaluation" idea in `docs/IDEAS.md` is correctly marked "Partially known"; the evidence above sharpens it: the Known baseline it must beat is abs(correction) scaling, not fixed margins.

### Gaps
- No public Elo figure was found for the individual Stockfish or Reckless patches that added abs(correction) to margins (the commits were not located).
- Whether uncertainty-scaled margins help more at ChessSI's depths (10+0.1, roughly depth 15-20) than at Stockfish's is untested.

- Order of work matters more than the list: the TT overhaul unlocks singular extensions and ProbCut; history persistence unlocks continuation history; correction history unlocks abs(corr) margins and novel candidates 1-3; the time-management rewrite unlocks candidate 5. High-gain items also pass SPRT fastest (about 2-5k games each), so the top five cost roughly a day or two of the 13-core share in total.
- Typical figures come from engines whose baselines differed from ChessSI's, so individual gains can be larger (ChessSI lacks many interacting features, as its history and LMP retests showed) or smaller (overlap, e.g. SEE pruning after LMP).
- The losing-captures-below-quiets idea in the queue has mixed evidence: Stash +17.9 per the queue note, but Alexandria's version did not pass (+1.47 ± 4.25).
