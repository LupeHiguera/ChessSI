# Self-play data generation, selection and labelling for ChessSI's NNUE: novel ideas transplanted from other game AI and ML

Research date: 2026-10-24. ChessSI facts come from `docs/IDEAS.md`, `nets/README.md` and `src/datagen.cpp` at commit
`2ab6bd2` (branch `claude/artifact-access-issue-ug96zp`), read without modifying the repo. Engine and trainer
code was read from shallow clones made the same day; links point at the commits read.

Context used to judge fit (from the ChessSI repo, read-only): `src/datagen.cpp` plays fixed-node self-play
(5,000 nodes a move) after 8 uniformly random plies, records only quiet positions (skips in-check,
noisy best move, mate scores), adjudicates a win at |score| >= 2500 cp held 6 plies, has no draw
adjudication (games run to 400 plies or a natural draw), and writes `fen | score | result`. net8 used
91.3M unique positions over 4 generations (37.2M PeSTO gen-1, 12.4M net4, 26.8M net6, 20.3M net7) plus
2.6M from SPRT games, all weighted equally. Validation loss on h256 stops improving after about 10
epochs at 23M positions: data, not network size, is the limit (`docs/IDEAS.md`, "NNUE training
measurements"). Already done or proposed (excluded from the candidate list): training-only auxiliary
head, SPRT games as data, KataGo playout-cap randomization, weighting by |static eval - search score|,
self-filtered opening book, GPU-teacher relabelling, self-distillation head, policy auxiliary head.

## 1. Known baseline: what top NNUE engines do for data generation today

### Takeaway
The open-data engines have converged on one recipe: fixed soft-node self-play (5k-25k soft nodes, a hard cap
8-40x higher), 8-9 random opening plies (often SEE-guarded or piece-weighted), a mix of DFRC and standard
starts, a verification search that throws away openings scoring beyond 500-1000 cp, win and draw
adjudication, and whole games stored in a binpack (viriformat) so that filtering happens at training time
(min ply about 16, no tactical best move, no check, optional WDL-consistency filter). Stockfish adds
training-time resampling: WDL-consistency skipping, soft early-ply skipping and a dynamic piece-count
target distribution. Stockfish, Alexandria and Obsidian train mainly on Lc0-derived data, which ChessSI's rule
forbids. Relabelling data with a network (Stockfish uses Lc0 BT4, Viridithas its own net) and
self-distillation (PlentyChess) are already practised.

### Cited Findings
Engine code was read from clones made 2026-10-24. Links are permalinks to the commit read.

- **Viridithas** (own data only; README: "All neural networks currently used in the development of Viridithas are trained exclusively on its own self-play games"): defaults are `nodes: 25_000` soft and `hard_limit: options.nodes * 8`, with `generate_dfrc: true`. The opening is `RANDOM_MOVES_ROOT = 8` random moves plus one more with probability 0.5. Each random move is retried up to 8 times to find one with SEE >= -1000 (`RANDOM_SEE_THRESHOLD`). A verification search of 50,000 soft nodes on the exit position discards it if |eval| > 1000. Win adjudication needs |score| >= 2500 for 4 plies; draw adjudication needs |score| <= 4 for 12 plies, at any ply. Each colour keeps its own 4 MB TT, and Syzygy WDL adjudication is optional. Whole games go to viriformat — [viridithas datagen.rs](https://github.com/cosmobobak/viridithas/blob/b407e3eb0b8fc6e251d22664b696cfa870208a7a/src/datagen.rs); [README](https://github.com/cosmobobak/viridithas/blob/b407e3eb0b8fc6e251d22664b696cfa870208a7a/README.md)
- **Viridithas training-time filter defaults:** `min_ply: 16`, `min_pieces: 4`, `max_eval: 20_000`, `filter_tactical: true`, `filter_check: true`. Optional settings are `max_opening_eval` and `max_eval_incorrectness`, which drops positions "where eval diverges from WDL by more than this value". There is also a `relabel` tool that overwrites every stored non-decisive score with a network's raw static evaluation — [viridithas dataformat.rs](https://github.com/cosmobobak/viridithas/blob/b407e3eb0b8fc6e251d22664b696cfa870208a7a/src/datagen/dataformat.rs); [datagen.rs relabel_batch](https://github.com/cosmobobak/viridithas/blob/b407e3eb0b8fc6e251d22664b696cfa870208a7a/src/datagen.rs)
- **Viridithas history:** early networks were trained on rescored self-play games. The README says "The 13th-generation network, and many since, include positions from the Lichess Elite dataset rescored by Viridithas" (human games with its own scores), and that current networks use its own self-play only — [viridithas README](https://github.com/cosmobobak/viridithas/blob/b407e3eb0b8fc6e251d22664b696cfa870208a7a/README.md)
- **Stormphrax** (README: "will never use data generated by a third party"): `kDatagenSoftNodeLimit = 24'000`, `kDatagenHardNodeLimit = 1'000'000`. Verification is at depth 10 (`kVerificationDepth`, 500k hard nodes) and discards openings with |score| > 500 (`kVerificationScoreLimit`). Win adjudication is |score| > 1250 for 5 plies. Draw adjudication is |score| < 10 for 10 plies, from ply 70. Games whose searches hit the hard node limit 3 times ("explosions") are thrown away. DFRC is optional (random index in 960 x 960), and the output can be marlinformat, viriformat or text — [Stormphrax datagen.cpp](https://github.com/Ciekce/Stormphrax/blob/d1468d99e3d3733100d20d682ac627998cce94b2/src/datagen/datagen.cpp); [README](https://github.com/Ciekce/Stormphrax/blob/d1468d99e3d3733100d20d682ac627998cce94b2/README.md)
- **PlentyChess:** README: "trained on 15+ billion self-generated chess positions (both standard and fischer random chess), using self-distillation techniques for part of the training". Its `genfens` opening generator plays 8-9 random moves, or 3-4 from a book. The mover is chosen by piece type: pawn 35%, knight 15%, bishop 15%, queen 15%, king 15%, rook 5%. A verification search of 1,000,000 nodes rejects |score| >= 1000. Games are then played by OpenBench DATAGEN workers — [PlentyChess datagen.cpp](https://github.com/Yoshie2000/PlentyChess/blob/9ca005740a35967c1403bfb9a3a9032c97ee003d/src/datagen.cpp); [README](https://github.com/Yoshie2000/PlentyChess/blob/9ca005740a35967c1403bfb9a3a9032c97ee003d/README.md)
- **Distributed datagen through OpenBench:** OpenBench "DATAGEN workloads" call the engine's `genfens N seed S book ...` to build openings. Games then run with options like `Threads=1 Hash=8 Minimal=true Normalize=false SoftNodes=true`, and scores are kept in the PGN move comments (Berserk's 2026-08-29 plan documents, written by a "Claude Agent") — [berserk datagen-genfens plan](https://github.com/jhonnold/berserk/blob/32628515050b83805bab4afa1026dd2bcaa93f55/docs/plans/2026-08-29-datagen-genfens.md); [berserk datagen-uci-options plan](https://github.com/jhonnold/berserk/blob/32628515050b83805bab4afa1026dd2bcaa93f55/docs/plans/2026-08-29-datagen-uci-options.md)
- **Lc0 data, not own data:** Alexandria "uses neural networks trained on data provided by the Leela Chess Zero project", and Obsidian "evaluates positions with a neural network trained on Lc0 data". Both train with bullet — [Alexandria README](https://github.com/PGG106/Alexandria/blob/54d4c206d76146fc88721b74f7ec65e077ab5dcb/README.md); [Obsidian README](https://github.com/gab8192/Obsidian/blob/720614a61ed6201037516d555f0bd2f7e5aeae3c/README.md)
- **bullet's recommended workflow:** store data in a binpack-like format, convert, shuffle each file, then interleave. Binpacks "store entire games contiguously ... and hence need a filter function". Viriformat is recommended as "the most commonly used amongst people who generate their own data" — [bullet docs/3-data.md](https://github.com/jw1912/bullet/blob/579b78b37a694cacd6e9ede4706386c15c34837c/docs/3-data.md)
- **Stockfish's own generator (historical):** `generate_training_data depth 9 ... random_multi_pv 4 random_multi_pv_diff 100 ... book noob_3moves.epd`. The wiki says "depth 9 still seems to be the sweet spot", that `nodes5000pv2_UHO` (5,000 nodes, MultiPV 2) "produces the best playing nets for the UHO books", and that `dfrc_n5000` was "generated with stockfish at 5000 nodes per move" from DFRC openings — [nnue-pytorch wiki: Training datasets](https://github.com/official-stockfish/nnue-pytorch/wiki/Training-datasets)
- **Stockfish on what makes data good:** "We don't really know ... Better evaluations don't always give better results, as it's a tradeoff between quality and learnability. Good datasets are chosen empirically." The wiki adds that Lc0-derived datasets "are of higher quality than the ones generated with Stockfish". The current pipeline relabels binpacks with an Lc0 BT4 network (`relabel_dual_stream_higher_batches`), and an older mix included "Stockfish self-play data from openings it usually gets wrong" — [nnue-pytorch wiki: Training datasets](https://github.com/official-stockfish/nnue-pytorch/wiki/Training-datasets)
- **Stockfish's training-time filters** (nnue-pytorch `DataloaderSkipConfig` defaults):
  - `filtered=True` skips capturing moves and positions in check.
  - `wld_filtered=True` skips a position with probability `1 - score_result_prob()`, i.e. when the score is unlikely given the game result.
  - `soft_early_fen_skipping=20` keeps early plies only with an interpolated probability.
  - `random_fen_skipping=0` turns random skipping off.
  - A dynamic piece-count filter resamples toward a spline target of relative frequency 0, 0.4, 1.0, 1.0 and 0.75 at 0, 8, 16, 24 and 32 pieces (`max_pc_skip_rate = 0.975`).
  — [nnue-pytorch data_loader/config.py](https://github.com/official-stockfish/nnue-pytorch/blob/ed84cc1fd2255faa0f33dcfa7839c9b6b4bb49b8/data_loader/config.py); [training_data_loader.cpp](https://github.com/official-stockfish/nnue-pytorch/blob/ed84cc1fd2255faa0f33dcfa7839c9b6b4bb49b8/data_loader/cpp/training_data_loader.cpp)

### Inferences
- ChessSI's datagen differs from the own-data engines (Viridithas, Stormphrax, PlentyChess) in six measurable ways:
  1. 5k hard nodes, against their 24-25k soft nodes. Soft limits finish the iteration, so labels are more consistent.
  2. Purely uniform random plies, with no SEE guard and no verification search, so some openings are already decided.
  3. No DFRC.
  4. No draw adjudication, so drawn games burn CPU to ply 400.
  5. One shared TT and searcher state between both sides.
  6. Filtering at write time, so dropped positions (checks, noisy best moves) are gone for good. The others store whole games and filter at training time.
- Fixing these is Known engineering, not research. It belongs in a "do first" backlog (see section 6), because the novel ideas should be measured on top of a sane baseline.
- Self-distillation (PlentyChess) and relabelling with a network (Viridithas `relabel`, Stockfish relabelling with Lc0 BT4) exist. ChessSI's "self-distillation head" and "GPU-teacher relabelling" proposals should therefore be logged as "Partially known", not Novel. ChessSI's training-only head variant may still be new.
- Stockfish's `wld_filtered` is the opposite of ChessSI's proposed |static eval - search| up-weighting. Stockfish *drops* positions whose score disagrees with the result. That is the prior art to cite and to compare against.

### Gaps
- Reckless's repository has no datagen code and its README does not state the data source, so its practice was not determined. Berserk's actual node count is set in OpenBench, not in its repo.
- Node counts used by OpenBench DATAGEN workloads for PlentyChess and Berserk are not in the repos read. The Engine Programming Discord, where most datagen tuning is discussed, could not be searched.

## 2. What from KataGo, Gumbel AlphaZero / MuZero, Lc0 and shogi engines has not been tried in NNUE chess datagen

### Takeaway
Go and MCTS self-play has many data techniques with no counterpart in the chess-NNUE generators read
(Viridithas, Stormphrax, PlentyChess, Stockfish tools, nnue-pytorch, bullet):

- KataGo: forks, side positions that refute bad alternatives, value- and policy-surprise frequency weighting, reduced visits and weight in lopsided positions, asymmetric playouts, a power-law replay window, and reanalysis of cheap-search positions.
- Lc0: deblunder (repairing the game-result target), q_ratio, diff-focus sampling, and KLD-gain adaptive visits.

Chess and shogi already have partial counterparts:

- Stockfish's `generate_training_data` puts up to 5 random moves anywhere in plies 1-24, chosen from MultiPV.
- Stockfish's `generate_training_data_nonpv` saves 1% of the positions visited inside the search tree and rescores them, which is DAgger-like.
- Shogi engines replace each training position with its qsearch PV leaf, and relabel NNUE-generated positions with a deep-learning teacher (distillation).

Gumbel AlphaZero/MuZero improves the policy with few simulations, which has little to offer a value-only alpha-beta NNUE.

### Cited Findings
- **KataGo self-play config (training/selfplay1.cfg):**
  - Forks: `earlyForkGameProb = 0.04` ("Fork to try alternative opening variety"), `forkGameProb = 0.01` ("Fork to try alternative crazy move anywhere in game ... if not early forking"), choosing "from the best of" 3-12 (early) or up to 36 random choices.
  - Side positions: `sidePositionProb = 0.020` ("With this probability, train on refuting bad alternative moves").
  - Cheap searches: `cheapSearchProb = 0.75`, `cheapSearchVisits = 100`, `cheapSearchTargetWeight = 0.0`, `maxVisits = 600`.
  - Reduced visits: `reduceVisits = true` when one side's winrate is > 0.9 for 3 turns, with `reducedVisitsMin = 100` and `reducedVisitsWeight = 0.1` ("Minimum training weight").
  - Surprise weighting: `policySurpriseDataWeight = 0.5` and `valueSurpriseDataWeight = 0.1` ("This proportion of training weight should be concentrated on surprising position results").
  - Asymmetric playouts: `normalAsymmetricPlayoutProb = 0.01` ("In regular games, play with unbalanced players").
  - Move temperature 0.75 early, decaying with a half-life of 19 moves to 0.15.

  — [KataGo selfplay1.cfg](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/cpp/configs/training/selfplay1.cfg)
- **KataGo code (clone at d91ea85, 2026-09-27):**
  - A side position is made by picking a random forking move from the network policy, excluding the move actually played; the position after it is searched later and recorded.
  - An optional `recordTreePositions` mode records subtree positions with enough visits "where the player to move made best moves along the tree".
  - Value surprise is the KL divergence of "the smoothed forward-looking game result" from the raw network's value. A `useSearchValueSurprise` switch compares the turn's own search value instead, "so that data weighting/selection based on it does not condition on the game's realized outcome".
  - `useReanalyze` and `reanalyzeProp` upgrade some cheap-search positions to training positions.

  — [KataGo play.cpp](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/cpp/program/play.cpp); [playsettings.cpp](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/cpp/program/playsettings.cpp)
- **KataGo methods doc on policy surprise weighting:** "about half of the total frequency weight is assigned uniformly, giving a baseline frequency weight of 0.5". The other half "is distributed proportionally to the KL-divergence from the policy prior". Surprising positions are sampled "more frequently, using full weight for the sample without scaling". KataGo "also add[s] a small weight for surprising utility value samples". The doc calls policy surprise weighting one of the larger improvements over earlier runs — [KataGoMethods.md](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/docs/KataGoMethods.md)
- **KataGo short-term value targets:** auxiliary heads "exponentially average future MCTS values" with mean horizons of about 6, 16 and 50 turns on 19x19. ChessSI's auxiliary head is the NNUE analogue and is already done — [KataGoMethods.md](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/docs/KataGoMethods.md)
- **Lc0 deblunder** (Tilps' `rescore_tb` branch, `selfplay/loop.cc`): the game is walked backwards from the end. "A blunder is defined by the played move being worse than the best move by a defined threshold, missing a forced win, or playing into a proven loss without being forced." At a blunder, the propagated result target (`result_q`, `result_d`, plies-left) is replaced by the searched value of the best move (`best_q`, `best_d`, `best_m`) for that position and every earlier one. An optional width averages old and new targets as a soft threshold — [Tilps/lc0 rescore_tb loop.cc](https://github.com/Tilps/lc0/blob/rescore_tb/src/selfplay/loop.cc)
- **Lc0 run history:** in T60, "68200: Deblunder started with threshold 0.1". T60 also introduced "value focus with min 0.7, slope 1.0", and later "'diff focus' with q_weight 6.0, pol_scale 3.5, focus slope 3.0, focus min 0.025". T52 introduced "Q_ratio where the training target becomes q_ratio • q + (1-q_ratio) • z". Self-play temperature was tuned constantly; for example, T70 set "Temp ... 0.9, endgame temp to 0.3, tempdecay-delay to 40, tempdecay to 30, temp cutoff to 60". Search length is adaptive through KLD-gain thresholds, e.g. "KLD set to 40 micronats" — [lc0 wiki Project-History](https://github.com/LeelaChessZero/lc0/wiki/Project-History)
- **Lc0 diff focus rule** (`lczero-training/tf/chunkparser.py`): a position is kept with probability `diff_focus_min + diff_focus_slope * (q_weight*|best_q - orig_q| + pol_kld)/(q_weight + pol_scale)`. Here `orig_q` is the raw network value and `best_q` the search value. This is a sampling version of ChessSI's proposed |static eval - search score| weighting — [lczero-training chunkparser.py](https://github.com/LeelaChessZero/lczero-training/blob/master/tf/chunkparser.py)
- **Stockfish `generate_training_data` defaults:** `random_move_min_ply` 1, `random_move_max_ply` 24, `random_move_count` 5. With `random_multi_pv`, "the random move will be one of the moves chosen by the search", limited by `random_multi_pv_diff` to moves "at most ... worse than the next best move". Other defaults: `write_min_ply` 16, `eval_limit` 3000, `adjudicate_draws_by_score` ("score remains 0 for at least 8 plies after ply 80"), `keep_draws` 1 — [Stockfish tools docs/generate_training_data.md](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/tools/docs/generate_training_data.md)
- **Stockfish `generate_training_data_nonpv`:** "plays fixed nodes self play games for exploration and records [some of] the evaluated positions. Then rescores them with fixed depth search". It uses `exploration_min_nodes` 5000 and `exploration_max_nodes` 15000, drawn uniformly. `exploration_save_rate` is 0.01, "meaning 1 in 100 positions seen during search get saved for rescoring", and rescoring `depth` defaults to 3 — [Stockfish tools docs/generate_training_data_nonpv.md](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/tools/docs/generate_training_data_nonpv.md)
- **Shogi (YaneuraOu wiki, translated from Japanese):**
  - The NNUE evaluation is called from positions where captures have settled in qsearch, so "training positions should be the qsearch leaf"; YaneuraOu's `qsearch_psv` command replaces each teacher position with the leaf of its qsearch PV.
  - "Evaluation values from YaneuraOu's normal search (NNUE etc.) are considered rough and noisy, so a DL model is preferable as the teacher." The recommended loop generates positions with NNUE self-play, relabels them with a dlshogi model and trains NNUE on the result (distillation).
  - Generating teacher positions "has no established method yet"; game records from shallow searches are most often used.
  - "Which loop is efficient has not been clarified."

  — [YaneuraOu wiki: 評価関数の学習](https://github.com/yaneurao/YaneuraOu/wiki/%E8%A9%95%E4%BE%A1%E9%96%A2%E6%95%B0%E3%81%AE%E5%AD%A6%E7%BF%92)
- **Shogi `eval_limit`:** the YaneuraOu changelog records that "it turned out to be better not to set eval_limit during teacher generation (use the upper limit 32000) [tanuki-(WCSC28)]", so keeping lopsided positions helped in shogi — [YaneuraOu wiki: 更新履歴2019まで](https://github.com/yaneurao/YaneuraOu/wiki/%E3%82%84%E3%81%AD%E3%81%86%E3%82%89%E7%8E%8B%E3%81%AE%E6%9B%B4%E6%96%B0%E5%B1%A5%E6%AD%B42019%E3%81%BE%E3%81%A7)
- **Chess-NNUE code search:** a grep for deblunder, forking or branching games, and position augmentation in Viridithas, Stormphrax, PlentyChess, bullet, nnue-pytorch and Stockfish tools found nothing. bullet's only "mirror" is the architectural "Horizontally mirrored, king bucketed psqt chess inputs" — clones of [bullet](https://github.com/jw1912/bullet), [viridithas](https://github.com/cosmobobak/viridithas), [Stormphrax](https://github.com/Ciekce/Stormphrax), [nnue-pytorch](https://github.com/official-stockfish/nnue-pytorch)

### Inferences
- **Not found in chess-NNUE datagen:** KataGo-style forks used to share a prefix between continuations; side positions that refute non-best moves; value-surprise frequency weighting; reduced-node, down-weighted play in lopsided positions; asymmetric-strength games; power-law replay windows; Lc0 deblunder; KLD-gain adaptive nodes. These are the transplant pool for section 6.
- **Not novel for ChessSI:**
  - Random moves in the first 24 plies chosen from MultiPV (Stockfish `random_multi_pv`).
  - Sampling positions from inside the search tree and rescoring them (Stockfish `generate_training_data_nonpv`).
  - qsearch-PV-leaf replacement (YaneuraOu).
  - Teacher relabelling and distillation (YaneuraOu/dlshogi, Stockfish relabelling with Lc0 BT4, Viridithas `relabel`, PlentyChess self-distillation).
- **Excluded proposals that now have closer prior art:**
  - |static eval - search| weighting: Lc0 diff focus, KataGo `useSearchValueSurprise`.
  - Playout-cap randomization: KataGo itself.
  - GPU-teacher relabelling and self-distillation: shogi distillation, PlentyChess.

  The ChessSI log should record these as Partially known.
- Lc0 deblunder cannot be copied as is. ChessSI plays the search's best move after ply 8, so "played_q < best_q" never happens. Every decisive result comes from a misjudgment, not exploration noise, and the λ=1.0 test (-80 Elo) says the result signal is worth keeping. Deblunder only makes sense together with deliberate exploration moves (candidate C in section 6).
- Gumbel AlphaZero/MuZero improves a *policy* with few simulations through sampling without replacement and sequential halving. A value-only NNUE in alpha-beta has no policy to improve. The only transplant is Gumbel-top-k sampling over MultiPV scores for exploratory moves, which is the same as Boltzmann or `random_multi_pv_diff` move choice (Known).

- arXiv, OpenReview, lczero.org, blog.lczero.org, chessprogramming.org and TalkChess are blocked by the proxy. KataGo's paper (arXiv 1902.10565), the Gumbel MuZero paper (Danihelka et al., ICLR 2022) and Lc0's blog posts on deblunder and diff focus could not be read. - No published ablation was found for Stockfish's `nonpv` generator or for its `random_multi_pv` settings in chess (Elo of nonpv-generated data vs game data).

## 3. What transfers from active learning, prioritised replay, hard-example mining and adversarial generation

### Takeaway
The literature splits three ways:

1. "Hard example" selection: prioritised replay, KataGo surprise weighting, Lc0 diff focus, and ChessSI's own |static - search| proposal.
2. "Clean, learnable example" selection: Stockfish's WDL filter, RHO-LOSS.
3. Distribution-level methods: prioritised start states (PLR, Go-Explore, AlphaZero puzzle starts), training on the states the learner actually visits (DAgger, Stockfish's nonpv generator, Giraffe's random-move perturbation), and learning from the whole search tree (TreeStrap).

For ChessSI's regime (small data, noisy 25% result target, overfitting after about 10 epochs), the data-pruning theory says to keep easy examples while data is scarce and hard ones once it is abundant. RHO-LOSS gives a principled middle path. Because the second PC moves ChessSI from scarce to abundant data within about a day, hard-example methods should be tested *after* the data jump, not before.

### Cited Findings
- **RHO-LOSS** (Mindermann et al., ICML 2022): "techniques from the optimization literature typically select 'hard' (e.g. high loss) points, but such points are often noisy (not learnable) or less task-relevant. Conversely, curriculum learning prioritizes 'easy' points, but such points need not be trained on once learned. In contrast, RHO-LOSS selects points that are learnable, worth learning, and not yet learnt." On Clothing-1M it "trains in 18x fewer steps and reaches 2% higher final accuracy than uniform data shuffling". It needs an "irreducible loss model" trained on holdout data — [OATML/RHO-Loss README](https://github.com/OATML/RHO-Loss) (paper: [PMLR v162](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/mindermann22a.html), not fetched: blocked)
- **Prioritized Level Replay** "adaptively updat[es] a sampling distribution over the training levels based on a score of the learning potential of replaying each level". Its examples use rank prioritisation and `--staleness_coef=0.1` — [facebookresearch/level-replay README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/level-replay) ([arXiv 2010.03934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.03934), not fetched)
- **Data pruning theory** (Sorscher et al. 2022): "when data is abundant (scarce) ... the better pruning strategy is to keep the hard (easy) examples". The same shift was confirmed on CIFAR-10 with ResNet18 — [arXiv 2206.14486](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14486) (search snippet; full text not fetched)
- **Prioritised experience replay in board-game self-play:** Soemers et al. 2020 apply PER within Expert Iteration across fourteen board games. They report "major improvements in early training performance in some games" but only minor improvements averaged over all fourteen — [arXiv 2006.00283](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.00283) (search snippet)
- **AlphaZero with curated start positions:** an AlphaZero study of creative chess starts self-play from a distribution mixing the standard start position with puzzle positions — [Zahavy et al., arXiv 2308.09175](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09175) (search snippet)
- **Giraffe (2015):** 5 million positions from computer games, each with one random legal move applied, gave 175 million training positions, so the evaluator would see the kinds of positions search reaches — [MIT Technology Review on Giraffe](https://www.technologyreview.com/2015/09/14/247956/deep-learning-machine-teaches-itself-chess-in-72-hours-plays-at-international-master/); [arXiv 1509.01549](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01549) (search snippets)
- **TreeStrap** (Veness, Silver, Uther, Blair, NIPS 2009) updates the heuristic toward alpha-beta search values at *all* nodes of the tree. The chess program Meep, starting from random weights, "played at a master level" against humans. The chessprogramming wiki's table gives TreeStrap(αβ) 2157 ± 31, TreeStrap(mm) 1807 ± 32, RootStrap(αβ) 1362 ± 59 and TD-Leaf 1068 ± 36 — [NIPS 2009 paper](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/3722-bootstrapping-from-game-tree-search); [chessprogramming wiki: Meep](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Meep) (via search snippets; both pages blocked for direct fetch)
- **TRPO's "vine" estimator** picks a "rollout set" of states along sampled trajectories. From each it runs several rollouts, using common random numbers to reduce variance — [Schulman et al., arXiv 1502.05477](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.05477) (search snippet)
- **Earlier ChessSI log entries (Astra):** search-contempt ([arXiv 2504.07757](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07757v1)) steers self-play toward positions a shallow opponent mishandles. Braun's preprint ([arXiv 2609.37447](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.37447v1)) found that a learned search-allocation selector improved label agreement but trailed controls by 60-100 Elo — [ChessSI docs/IDEAS.md, "Novel ideas"](https://github.com/LupeHiguera/ChessSI/blob/main/docs/IDEAS.md)
- **Production hard-example weighting:** KataGo puts 50% of frequency weight on policy surprise and 10% on value surprise. Lc0 T60 used diff focus with min 0.025 and slope 3.0. Stockfish's `wld_filtered` drops positions whose score is unlikely given the result, which is the opposite direction (sources in sections 1 and 2).

### Inferences
- **Disagreement sampling** (shallow vs deep search, or static eval vs search) is the same family as Lc0 diff focus and KataGo value surprise. For ChessSI it is "Partially known" either way. The open question is the direction: up-weight like KataGo and Lc0, or drop like Stockfish. The answer probably depends on the data regime (Sorscher). Test both directions against uniform on the same frozen data, after the data jump.
- **Uncertainty oversampling:** Braun's result is a warning that biased selection can raise label agreement and still lose Elo. Any directed selection, whether of positions to deepen or to oversample, should be importance-weighted (sample with probability q(x) and weight by 1/q(x)) or mixed with a uniform floor, as KataGo's 0.5 uniform baseline does. It should only be accepted on an equal-cost SPRT.
- **Fewer positions per game:** recording a position costs no extra search, since the move is searched anyway. Skipping positions saves CPU only if unrecorded moves get cheaper searches, which is the playout-cap randomization ChessSI already proposed. Stockfish now ships `random_fen_skipping=0`. More important is that the result label is shared by about 84 positions per game: 91.3M positions from 1.09M games, per `nets/README.md`. The number of independent *results* is the number of games, which motivates candidate A (branched games) in section 6.
- **Adversarial generation:** the feasible own-data version is replaying ChessSI's own hard positions as start states (PLR; candidate G). Synthetic positions optimised to fool the evaluator would be off-distribution, and no chess evidence for them was found.

### Gaps
- No chess or NNUE use of RHO-LOSS, PLR or importance-weighted active learning was found. Discord could not be searched, so absence is not proof.
- The KataGo adversarial-position training (after the 2022-23 "adversarial policies" papers) could not be verified. `hintPosesProb` is commented out in the default self-play config.
- The DAgger paper (Ross et al. 2011, [arXiv 1011.0686](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0686)) and Schaul et al.'s prioritised replay ([arXiv 1511.05952](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05952)) could not be fetched. They are cited for the concept only.

## 4. Quantity versus quality: 5,000 nodes versus 10-20k nodes or deeper rescoring

### Takeaway
No controlled, equal-CPU public comparison of 5k-node against 10-20k-node chess-NNUE data was found. Practice ranges from 5k nodes (Stockfish's `nodes5000` and `dfrc_n5000` sets, many hobby engines) to 24-25k *soft* nodes (Viridithas, Stormphrax), which generate billions of positions for much larger networks. ChessSI's own history suggests about 75-135 Elo per doubling of data so far. At equal CPU, doubling nodes (half the positions) would need a label-quality gain of that size, which is implausible at 0.1B positions. So stay at 5k (soft) for now.

The second PC changes the regime within about a day (0.7-1.4B positions a day at 30-60M an hour). Past roughly 0.5-1B positions the h256 network should saturate. Label quality, and larger networks, then become the better buy. Run one equal-CPU 5k-vs-10k test once that scale is reached, not before.

### Cited Findings
- Stockfish wiki: "depth 9 still seems to be the sweet spot" for Stockfish-generated data. `nodes5000pv2_UHO` "produces the best playing nets for the UHO books". "Better evaluations don't always give better results, as it's a tradeoff between quality and learnability" — [nnue-pytorch wiki: Training datasets](https://github.com/official-stockfish/nnue-pytorch/wiki/Training-datasets)
- Own-data engines: Viridithas defaults to 25,000 soft nodes (hard cap 8x) and Stormphrax to 24,000 soft (1,000,000 hard). Stockfish's nonpv generator explores at 5,000-15,000 nodes and rescores saved positions at depth 3 — [viridithas datagen.rs](https://github.com/cosmobobak/viridithas/blob/b407e3eb0b8fc6e251d22664b696cfa870208a7a/src/datagen.rs); [Stormphrax datagen.cpp](https://github.com/Ciekce/Stormphrax/blob/d1468d99e3d3733100d20d682ac627998cce94b2/src/datagen/datagen.cpp); [SF nonpv docs](https://github.com/official-stockfish/Stockfish/blob/tools/docs/generate_training_data_nonpv.md)
- 5,000 nodes is a common default among hobby engines. One chess-programming blog relays a Discord recommendation of "a 5000 node limit" for datagen. Search results found no head-to-head node-count study — [dogeystamp: Chess engine pt. 6](https://www.dogeystamp.com/chess6/) (search snippet)
- KataGo's answer to the same trade-off is playout-cap randomization. 25% of moves get full 600-visit searches and are recorded; 75% get 100-visit cheap searches and are not recorded (`cheapSearchTargetWeight = 0.0`) — [KataGo selfplay1.cfg](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/cpp/configs/training/selfplay1.cfg)
- Repeated data: in language models, up to 4 epochs of repeated data is almost as good as unique data at fixed compute. Returns diminish beyond that, and 40 epochs is "essentially worthless" — [Muennighoff et al., NeurIPS 2023](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/9d89448b63ce1e2e8dc7af72c984c196-Abstract.html) (search snippet)
- ChessSI's measurements:
  - Successive networks gained +138.6 (2.2x data, of which the auxiliary head alone was +53.2), +103.7 (1.7x) and +43.1 (1.4x).
  - h256 validation stops improving after about 10 epochs at 23M positions.
  - "Data, not network size, is the limit."
  - Wider networks are "only slightly better at 64.6M positions".
  - King buckets are planned "only past ~1B positions".

  — [ChessSI docs/IDEAS.md](https://github.com/LupeHiguera/ChessSI/blob/main/docs/IDEAS.md); [nets/README.md](https://github.com/LupeHiguera/ChessSI/blob/main/nets/README.md)

### Inferences
- **Elo per doubling (computed here):** +85.4 for 2.2x (aux head removed) is 75 Elo per doubling; +103.7 for 1.7x is 136; +43.1 for 1.4x is 89. These overstate pure quantity, because each step also added data from a stronger generation. They still set the bar: at equal CPU, 10k-node data halves the positions and must buy roughly 75-135 Elo of label quality to break even.
- **Supporting signs that quantity wins at this scale:**
  - λ 0.6 vs 0.75 was level. Small changes in target noise move little.
  - Stockfish found better labels are not always better ("learnability").
  - ChessSI repeats its data 20 times. Muennighoff's result suggests unique data is worth much more than another epoch.
- **Recommended split for the second PC:**
  1. Now to about 0.5B positions: 5k soft nodes (finish the iteration; hard cap 8x), as many unique positions as possible. Optionally test the already proposed playout-cap randomization: 5k recorded, about 1k unrecorded.
  2. At 0.5-1B positions, with a larger network: an equal-CPU test of 5k vs 10k soft nodes from the same network. Split the PC 10/10 cores for about 3 hours, train two otherwise identical networks on old data plus each new set, then SPRT.
  3. If 10k wins, move to 10-20k soft for later generations, matching Viridithas and Stormphrax at their scale.
- **Deeper rescoring of existing data** costs about one search per position, the same as generating a new one, but adds no new positions or results. Use it only for (a) positions flagged as likely mislabelled (candidate H) and (b) a fixed "gold" validation set: about 200k held-out positions at about 200k nodes, roughly 4e10 nodes. That is about 8-16 minutes of the second PC at the stated 30-60M positions an hour (1.5-3e11 nodes an hour at 5k nodes per position).
- **Why the gold set matters:** ChessSI found that validation loss is not comparable across label sets or λ. A gold set fixes that, so datagen recipes (node counts, filters, candidates in section 6) can be pre-screened by the trained network's gold-set loss before an SPRT. Braun's caution applies: confirm with games.

### Gaps
- No published Elo-at-equal-CPU comparison of node counts for chess-NNUE datagen was found. The Engine Programming Discord, where such numbers are usually posted, is not searchable.
- The CPU cost per position of ChessSI's datagen (NPS at 5k nodes on the 265K) is not stated in the repo files read. The 30-60M an hour figure is taken from the brief.

## 5. Mixing data from earlier generations: replay window and recency weighting

### Takeaway
Go and MCTS systems train on a sliding window of recent data. KataGo's window grows sublinearly as a power law; at ChessSI's 91.3M rows it would keep only the newest 7.2M (7.9%). Chess-NNUE projects instead accumulate everything and interleave it, or train in stages: Stockfish trains first on its own data, then retrains on Lc0 data. They bring old data up to date with tools (Viridithas `rescale` and `relabel`; Stockfish relabelling with Lc0 BT4).

No NNUE ablation of window size or recency weighting was found. For ChessSI the concrete question is the 37.2M PeSTO-era gen-1 positions, 41% of net8's data. Three free tests answer it: down-weighting or dropping them, per-generation score calibration, and newest-generation fine-tuning. The question fades on its own as the second PC adds about 1B positions a day.

### Cited Findings
- **KataGo window:** `WINDOWSIZE(N) = (N^EXPONENT - MIN_ROWS^EXPONENT) / (EXPONENT * MIN_ROWS^(EXPONENT-1)) * INITIAL_WINDOW_PER_ROW + MIN_ROWS`. Main runs used "-taper-window-exponent 0.65 or 0.675", "-expand-window-per-row 0.4" and "-min-rows 250000". The script "shuffles the most recent window of data" — [KataGo python/shuffle.py](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/python/shuffle.py); [python/selfplay/shuffle.sh](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/python/selfplay/shuffle.sh)
- **Stockfish dataset order:** "the order of the datasets trained with matters. It is usually best to first train a network with datasets generated with Stockfish (depth 9, nodes 5000), and then retrain an already good network using various Lc0-derived datasets" — [nnue-pytorch wiki: Training datasets](https://github.com/official-stockfish/nnue-pytorch/wiki/Training-datasets)
- **Viridithas:** "Subsequent networks were trained on additional self-play games by subsequent versions of Viridithas", i.e. cumulative data. It ships `run_rescale(input, output, scale)` to rescale stored evaluations and `relabel` to overwrite them with a network's evaluation — [viridithas README](https://github.com/cosmobobak/viridithas/blob/b407e3eb0b8fc6e251d22664b696cfa870208a7a/README.md); [datagen.rs](https://github.com/cosmobobak/viridithas/blob/b407e3eb0b8fc6e251d22664b696cfa870208a7a/src/datagen.rs)
- **bullet's workflow** shuffles each file, then interleaves all files — [bullet docs/3-data.md](https://github.com/jw1912/bullet/blob/579b78b37a694cacd6e9ede4706386c15c34837c/docs/3-data.md)
- **ChessSI:** net8 weights 37.2M gen-1 (PeSTO), 12.4M gen-2 (net4), 26.8M gen-3 (net6), 20.3M gen-4 (net7) and 2.6M SPRT-game positions equally. Deterministic keys remove 10.19% duplicates, and validation games are now chosen by a stable per-game key, which fixes cross-generation validation contamination — [ChessSI nets/README.md](https://github.com/LupeHiguera/ChessSI/blob/main/nets/README.md); [docs/IDEAS.md](https://github.com/LupeHiguera/ChessSI/blob/main/docs/IDEAS.md)

### Inferences
- **KataGo's window at NNUE sizes**, computed from its formula with min_rows 250k, exponent 0.65 and 0.4 per row: 17.5M rows gives 2.5M (14.5%), 91.3M gives 7.2M (7.9%), 300M gives 15.5M (5.2%), 1B gives 33.9M (3.4%).
- **Why that window is probably too short for ChessSI:** it exists because stale policy and value targets from weak networks hurt a large network that sees the same windowed data many times. ChessSI's h256 value network is data-starved. A full KataGo window would throw away most of its data, so a direct copy is unwise. The transferable part is the *principle*: old generations should count for less, not equally. The weighting should be measured, not assumed.
- **Free tests on the net9 snapshot** (same seed and epochs; stable per-game validation key; SPRT the best against (a)):
  - (a) uniform weighting (current);
  - (b) gen-1 dropped;
  - (c) recency weights 1.0 for the newest two generations and SPRT games, 0.5 for net6 data, 0.25 for net4 data, 0.125 for PeSTO data;
  - (d) uniform, plus 2-4 final epochs on the newest generation only (Stockfish-style staging).
- **Per-generation calibration:** fit each generation's score-to-result sigmoid scale by logistic regression of the game result on the stored score, then rescale scores to one common scale before training. PeSTO-search scores and NNUE-search scores are unlikely to share a calibration with eval scale 400. This is Partially known (Viridithas `rescale`, Stockfish's WDL normalisation) and costs minutes.
- **Once the second PC runs,** each new day of data outnumbers all earlier generations by about 10x, so mixing becomes a minor issue. Old generations matter mainly as a diversity reserve, at low weight.

### Gaps
- Lc0's window sizes (games or chunks per run) could not be verified: the lczero.org pages are blocked and the GitHub wiki copy does not list them.
- No NNUE engine was found to publish window-size or recency-weighting ablations.

### Inferences

#### A. Branched ("vine") self-play: fork each game and average the shared prefix's result (rank 1)
- **Idea:** at a random mid-game ply f (say uniform over 16-100), fork the game into K = 2 continuations and play each to the end. Every position is still searched and recorded exactly once. Prefix positions (plies < f) get the *mean* of the K results as their result target; each branch's positions keep their own result.
  - Sources: TRPO's vine estimator (rollout sets with multiple rollouts and common random numbers, [arXiv 1502.05477](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.05477)); KataGo's forks.
  - Byproduct: the same averaging applies to the 10% of positions that dedup currently discards. Average their results instead of keeping one copy.
- **Prior art: Novel in chess NNUE.** No forking or branching code was found in any chess datagen read. KataGo forks only for diversity: its fork is a separate game, and the main game's result targets are not averaged. TRPO uses vines for policy-gradient advantage estimates, not for value labels in self-play. The Engine Programming Discord could not be searched.
- **Why it could help ChessSI:**
  - The result target matters: λ = 1.0 lost -80.4 Elo.
  - It is the noisiest part of the target. It is a single {0, 0.5, 1} draw shared by about 84 positions per game, so only 1.09M independent outcomes stand behind 91.3M positions.
  - Averaging K branches cuts the after-fork part of that noise by up to K times for every prefix position, at no extra search cost.
  - The +35 to +53 Elo of the auxiliary head came from adding a *cleaner* target. This is a second route to the same effect.
  - Forks also add diversity in the middlegame, where ChessSI has none after ply 8.
- **Expected Elo:** 0 to +25, central +8, low confidence. The effect scales with how often two continuations from the same position end differently. The pre-check below measures exactly that.
- **Cost:**
  - About 1 day in `src/datagen.cpp`: keep the move list, rebuild the position at f, call `Search::clear()` (a fresh TT is the natural perturbation) or jitter nodes by about 1%, then write the prefix with the averaged result.
  - Pre-check: about 20 core-minutes.
  - Main test: about 6 hours of the second PC split 10/10 cores at equal CPU, 3 networks (about 1 GPU-hour), and 1-2 SPRTs (2-6k games each).
- **Minimal experiment:**
  1. Probe. From 2,000 positions at plies 20-80 of existing datagen games, play two continuations each (TT cleared, 5,000 vs 5,050 nodes). Record the share whose results differ and the correlation of the two outcomes. Below about 10% disagreement, stop: averaging buys little. If the branches rarely diverge in moves, force divergence with the second-best root move when it is within 30 cp.
  2. Generate equal-CPU datasets: control (current datagen), vine-avg, and vine-noavg (same games, each branch keeps its own result for the prefix).
  3. Train net9 variants on the old data plus each new set with the same seed. The difference vine-avg minus vine-noavg isolates the averaging; vine-noavg minus control isolates the distribution change.
  4. SPRT the best against control with [0, 5].
  - Use the tree root's key as the validation key for every branch, so the shared prefix never straddles the train/validation split.
- **Risks:**
  - Branch outcomes may be highly correlated, giving less than K-fold variance reduction.
  - There are fewer distinct early positions per CPU hour.
  - Branches that replay the same moves produce duplicates (wasted searches). Node jitter may not diverge quickly; the 30 cp second-move rule fixes this but slightly biases the prefix result.
  - Writing branches as separate "games" must not break the auxiliary head's "N plies later" lookup or the per-game validation split.

#### B. Mirror augmentation of castling-free positions (rank 2)
- **Idea:** with no castling rights, chess rules are exactly symmetric under the a-h file mirror. Each training position whose FEN castling field is "-" is mirrored with probability 0.5 per epoch (files a<->h, en-passant square included), with labels unchanged. ChessSI's 768 inputs carry no castling information, so the network gets a second, distinct input vector with an exactly correct label.
- **Prior art: Partially known.**
  - AlphaGo Zero used all 8 symmetries in Go. AlphaZero explicitly did not augment chess because its rules are asymmetric, and did not exploit the castling-free subset, which is symmetric.
  - Chess NNUE uses mirroring as *architecture*: Stockfish's HalfKAv2_hm, bullet's mirrored king-bucket inputs, Leorik. ChessSI's NNUE track already lists "horizontal mirroring" as Known.
  - No *augmentation* of castling-free positions was found in bullet, nnue-pytorch, Viridithas or Stormphrax.
- **Why it could help ChessSI:** it is data-limited (overfits after about 10 epochs; data "is the limit"), and augmentation is the standard remedy, at zero CPU. Random flipping keeps the position distribution unchanged.
- **Expected Elo:** 0 to +20, central +6, low-moderate confidence. Most at today's 0.1B positions, less as data passes 1B.
- **Cost:** 2-4 hours in `nnue/train.py`; 1-2 networks (15-30 GPU-minutes each); 1 SPRT.
- **Minimal experiment:**
  1. Count the castling-free share of net8's 91.3M positions. If it is under about 40%, expect little.
  2. Train net8's recipe with and without flipping on the same frozen snapshot and seed.
  3. Compare validation loss on unflipped held-out games. This is comparable, because the labels are the same.
  4. SPRT [0, 5] if it is lower.
- **Risks:**
  - It becomes redundant if ChessSI adopts mirrored (HM) features or king buckets.
  - Asymmetric tie-breaks in move ordering make labels only near-symmetric (negligible).
  - Positions with castling rights must be excluded, or their labels are wrong.

#### C. Result weight that depends on plies to the game's end (TD(λ)-style target) (rank 3)
- **Idea:** keep the target = (1 - w)·σ(score) + w·result, but make w a function of the plies d remaining in the game, e.g. w(d) ∝ exp(-d/τ) + c, normalised so the mean w stays 0.25. The result then informs late positions more and early ones less. ChessSI's files keep games contiguous, so d is known.
  - Sources: TD(λ), TD-Leaf (KnightCap, Giraffe), KataGo's horizon-weighted short-term targets, Lc0's constant q_ratio.
- **Prior art: Partially known.** TD(λ) blends are classic. But nnue-pytorch and bullet schedule λ only over training time, and no per-position, plies-to-end λ was found.
- **Why it could help ChessSI:** the λ sweep (0.6 level with 0.75; 1.0 at -80) measured *how much* result to use, but not *where*. A result 150 plies away carries mostly noise from later mistakes, while one 10 plies away is nearly decided.
- **Expected Elo:** -5 to +15, central +3, low-moderate confidence. The optimum may be flat, as the λ 0.6 test suggests.
- **Cost:** 1-2 hours in the trainer; 2-3 networks (τ = 20, 40, 80 plies); 1-2 SPRTs.
- **Minimal experiment:**
  1. On held-out games, measure how well σ(score) predicts the result (log-loss) in plies-to-end buckets. If predictability is flat in d, drop the idea.
  2. Otherwise train the τ variants against net8's recipe on the same data and seed. Validation loss is not comparable across targets, so SPRT the most promising τ against control.
- **Risks:**
  - Overlap with the auxiliary head, which already supplies a short-horizon target.
  - Win-adjudicated games end 6 plies early, distorting d near the end.
  - The aux-head ablation found that later scores estimate the current value worse, so do not also swap in future scores.

#### D. Mid-game exploration moves with Lc0-style deblundered results (rank 4)
- **Idea:** after ply 16, with probability p of about 0.04 per move, choose uniformly among root moves within Δ of about 40 cp of the best, using MultiPV-3 scores or a second search that excludes the best move. When writing, walk backwards from the end as Lc0's deblunder does. At each exploration move whose win-probability loss (WDL of the best score minus WDL of the played score) exceeds θ of about 0.05, replace the result target of that position and all earlier ones with WDL(best score), optionally with Lc0's soft width.
- **Prior art: Partially known.**
  - Stockfish's generator explores with `random_multi_pv` / `random_multi_pv_diff`, up to 5 random moves in plies 1-24, but without repairing results.
  - KataGo forks "anywhere in game" (1%).
  - Lc0 deblunders temperature moves.
  - No chess-NNUE generator combines mid-game exploration with deblundered results.
- **Why it could help ChessSI:**
  - After ply 8, its games are deterministic best-move play, so the positions are on-trajectory.
  - The search spends most of its evaluations on positions after inferior moves. Near-best alternatives move data toward that distribution.
  - Deblunder stops the exploration from polluting the result labels of every earlier position. That pollution is why exploration usually stays confined to the opening.
- **Expected Elo:** -5 to +15, central +4, low confidence.
- **Cost:** 1-2 days (exclusion search or MultiPV, plus the backward writer pass); +3-5% CPU; equal-CPU datasets (about 6 hours of the split second PC); 2 networks; 1 SPRT.
- **Minimal experiment:** one equal-CPU pair (control vs exploration+deblunder), trained on old data plus each set with the same seed, then SPRT. A third set, exploration *without* deblunder, shows whether the repair matters.
- **Risks:**
  - Within 40 cp, exploration may barely change games; wider margins cost results.
  - θ needs tuning.
  - ChessSI's search may need MultiPV support first.

#### E. Covariate-shift reweighting toward the positions the search actually evaluates (rank 5)
- **Idea:** ChessSI trains on quiet positions from best-play trajectories, but the evaluation is called mostly at qsearch stand-pats and in refuted lines.
  1. Sample about 1 in 1,000 evaluation calls during a few hundred ChessSI dev-vs-dev games at 10+0.1, giving 5-10M positions.
  2. Train a small classifier (logistic regression or a tiny MLP on the 768 features plus phase) to tell those positions from training positions.
  3. Weight each training position by the clipped density ratio p/(1 - p), clipped to [0.2, 5], with mean 1.
  - A "decision-relevant" variant samples only evaluation calls within about 100 cp of the alpha-beta bound, where the evaluation actually decides a cutoff.
- **Prior art: Partially known.** Every existing fix is hand-tuned or generative: Stockfish's piece-count spline, Stockfish's `nonpv` generator, YaneuraOu's qsearch-leaf replacement, Giraffe's random-move perturbation. No learned density-ratio weighting was found for NNUE.
- **Why it could help ChessSI:** it directly targets the mismatch between where labels come from and where evaluation accuracy is used, with no new search.
- **Expected Elo:** -5 to +15, central +4, low confidence.
- **Cost:** 1-2 days; 1-2 core-hours of games; about 1 GPU-hour; 1 SPRT.
- **Minimal experiment:**
  1. First a diagnostic: compare marginals of piece count, material imbalance, ply and check status between search-evaluated and training positions. If they match closely, drop the idea.
  2. If the search distribution has mass where training data has none, reweighting cannot help. Then the Known fix is Stockfish-style `nonpv` sampling with rescoring.
  3. Otherwise train weighted vs unweighted on the same snapshot and seed, then SPRT.
- **Risks:**
  - "Where the evaluation is called" is not "where accuracy matters": many calls are in lines that get refuted at once. The decision-relevant variant addresses this.
  - Weights reduce the effective sample size in a data-limited regime.

#### F. RHO-LOSS selection, with ChessSI networks trained on held-out halves as the irreducible-loss models (rank 6)
- **Idea:**
  1. Split games by stable hash into halves H1 and H2, and train two quick h256 networks, one per half (about 5 epochs, about 10 GPU-minutes each).
  2. For every position, store the loss of the network that did *not* see it. This is the irreducible-loss estimate: high means a noisy or unlearnable label.
  3. In main training, draw candidate batches 4x larger and keep the 25% with the highest (current loss minus irreducible loss).
  - A softer variant only down-weights positions with high irreducible loss. That is a learned version of Stockfish's WDL filter.
- **Prior art: Novel in chess NNUE.** RHO-LOSS is from ML (ICML 2022). Nothing similar was found in nnue-pytorch, bullet, or Lc0's chunk parser, which only has diff focus.
- **Why it could help ChessSI:** the hard-versus-clean question (KataGo and Lc0 up-weight surprises; Stockfish drops score/result contradictions) has a principled answer here. Train on what is learnable and not yet learnt, which suits noisy result labels.
- **Expected Elo:** -5 to +15, central +3, low confidence. RHO-LOSS's gains are measured on noisy web classification, not value regression.
- **Cost:** about 1 day in the trainer; 2-3x GPU per epoch (under an hour at current sizes); 1 SPRT.
- **Minimal experiment:** net9 recipe vs net9+RHO-LOSS on the same snapshot and seed. Check validation loss on the stable held-out games (comparable: same labels), then SPRT.
- **Risks:**
  - Selection shrinks the effective data while data is scarce; Sorscher says keep easy examples in that regime.
  - It may over-focus on some game phases.
  - The irreducible-loss models must never have seen the positions they score.

#### G. Prioritised start-state replay (PLR) from ChessSI's own games (rank 7)
- **Idea:** keep a pool of quiet positions at plies 12-60 from ChessSI's *own* datagen and dev-vs-dev SPRT games, not games against other engines.
  - Score each by learning potential: |current network's static WDL - smoothed outcome|, or net8/net7 disagreement.
  - Start about 25% of datagen games from the pool, sampled by rank-based priority (temperature 0.1) with PLR's staleness term (0.1), followed by 2 SEE-guarded random plies.
  - The rest use the normal 8 random plies.
- **Prior art: Partially known.**
  - Stockfish's dataset mix included "Stockfish self-play data from openings it usually gets wrong".
  - AlphaZero's creative-chess study mixed puzzle start positions into self-play.
  - KataGo has `startPoses` and `hintPoses` (off in the default config).
  - PER in Expert Iteration gave only minor average gains over 14 games.
  - PLR's adaptive, staleness-aware scheduler has not been applied to chess datagen as far as found.
- **Why it could help ChessSI:** it concentrates new games where the current network is wrong, instead of only re-weighting positions already recorded (the excluded proposal).
- **Expected Elo:** -10 to +15, central +2, low confidence.
- **Cost:** about 1 day; equal CPU; 1 SPRT.
- **Minimal experiment:** an equal-CPU pair (25% pool starts vs none), train, then SPRT. Also log whether pool-started games really yield higher-loss positions for the current network.
- **Risks:**
  - Distribution bias toward odd or tactical positions that may be unlearnable (a RHO-style filter on the pool helps).
  - Feedback loops across generations.
  - Braun's warning that targeted selection can lose Elo.

#### H. Selective deep relabelling triggered by search instability (active label cleaning) (rank 8)
- **Idea:** flag likely-wrong labels from signals the datagen search already produces:
  - the root best move changed in the last iteration, or the score moved by more than 50 cp between the last two iterations;
  - a large TD error between consecutive plies (|s_t + s_{t+1}| > 150 cp).
  Re-search only the flagged 5-10% at 8x nodes, replacing the score and keeping the result.
- **Prior art: Partially known.**
  - Lc0 adapts visits per move by KLD-gain convergence.
  - ChessSI's excluded |static - search| proposal and Lc0 diff focus target *hard* positions; this targets *unreliable labels*.
  - Braun's preprint shows selective deep labelling can lose to controls.
- **Why it could help ChessSI:** 5k hard-node labels are noisiest exactly where the search was still changing its mind. Deeper labels there buy more accuracy per node than uniform deeper search.
- **Expected Elo:** -10 to +10 at equal CPU, central +2, low confidence.
- **Cost:** about 1 day; +40-80% CPU per dataset (the control gets the same CPU as extra plain positions); 1 SPRT.
- **Minimal experiment:**
  1. Survey 100k positions for the flag rate and the mean |label change| when flagged positions are re-searched at 40k nodes. If changes are small, stop.
  2. Otherwise run an equal-CPU pair, train, then SPRT.
- **Risks:**
  - Flagged positions are tactical, and their deep labels may be unlearnable for h256.
  - The equal-CPU comparison is likely to favour plain data while data is scarce.

#### I. Censored labels from the search's fail-low bounds (TreeStrap(αβ) transplant) (rank 9)
- **Idea:** each 5k-node search already proves upper bounds for non-best root moves (fail-lows at depth d).
  - Record, for 2-3 such moves per search with d >= 4, the child position and the bound "value for the root mover <= alpha".
  - Train them with a one-sided loss that penalises the network only when its WDL for that child exceeds σ(alpha), with no result term.
  - This gives off-trajectory, "this move is worse" supervision at almost no search cost.
- **Prior art: Partially known.**
  - TreeStrap(αβ) (Veness et al. 2009, Meep) trains a linear evaluation toward alpha-beta values and bounds at all tree nodes. The chessprogramming wiki table rates it 2157 vs TD-Leaf 1068.
  - KataGo's `recordTreePositions` (off by default) and Stockfish's `nonpv` generator record exact values from the tree.
  - Bound-only (censored) labels for NNUE were not found.
- **Why it could help ChessSI:** many free labels from the distribution the search sees.
- **Expected Elo:** -5 to +10, central +2, low confidence.
- **Cost:** about 2 days (search hook plus loss); CPU about 0; GPU +30-50% samples; 1 SPRT.
- **Minimal experiment:**
  1. Survey the depth distribution of fail-low root moves in datagen searches. If most are below 4 after LMR, stop.
  2. Otherwise train with and without the censored samples, then SPRT.
- **Risks:**
  - Bounds from heavily reduced late-move searches are weak.
  - Many such children are trivially lost.
  - One-sided losses can shift the evaluation scale.
  - The extra sample volume is low in information.

#### Known baseline backlog (do first; Known, not research)
1. **Soft node limit** with a hard cap of about 8x (Viridithas 25k soft / 8x hard; Stormphrax 24k / 1M). Finishing the iteration makes labels more consistent.
2. **Draw adjudication.** Practice:
   - Viridithas: |score| <= 4 for 12 plies.
   - Stormphrax: |score| < 10 for 10 plies after ply 70.
   - Stockfish: score 0 for 8 plies after ply 80.

   ChessSI plays to 400 plies, so first measure the share of plies spent in long drawn tails.
3. **Opening hygiene:**
   - A verification search on the exit position: Viridithas 50k nodes rejecting |eval| > 1000; Stormphrax depth 10 rejecting > 500; PlentyChess 1M nodes rejecting >= 1000.
   - SEE-guarded random moves (Viridithas, SEE >= -1000, 8 retries).
4. **Store whole games and filter at training time** (viriformat-style). This brings in Stockfish's `wld_filtered`, soft early-ply skipping and piece-count reshaping as switchable options. It also lets currently-dropped positions (in check, noisy best move) come back through YaneuraOu-style qsearch-PV-leaf replacement.
5. **A DFRC share** for diversity (Viridithas default DFRC, Stockfish `dfrc_n5000`, PlentyChess FRC). Keep standard-chess games the majority, since all tests are standard chess.
6. **Minor:** one TT per side in datagen games (Viridithas). KataGo-style reduced nodes and 0.1 weight in lopsided positions, combined with the already-proposed playout-cap randomization.
7. **Generation mixing and calibration tests** from section 5.

#### Prior-art corrections for ChessSI's already-proposed ideas (for the log's Kind column)
- **|static eval - search| weighting:** Lc0 diff focus (sampling by |best_q - orig_q| plus policy KL) and KataGo value surprise (`useSearchValueSurprise`). Stockfish's `wld_filtered` is the opposite direction. Partially known.
- **Self-distillation head:** PlentyChess trains "using self-distillation techniques for part of the training". Shogi distils dlshogi models into NNUE. Partially known; the training-only *head* form may still be new.
- **GPU-teacher relabelling:**
  - Shogi's recommended loop relabels NNUE-generated positions with a deep-learning teacher.
  - Stockfish relabels binpacks with Lc0 BT4.
  - Viridithas `relabel` writes a network's evaluation into stored data.
  - Known in principle.
- **Playout-cap randomization:** KataGo, already labelled a transplant.

### Gaps
- None of the nine candidates has any chess Elo evidence. The ranges are priors from mechanism and from ChessSI's own effect sizes (data scaling, auxiliary head, λ sweep), and should be treated as such.
- The novelty verdicts rest on code reads of seven open engines and trainers, plus web searches. Discord, TalkChess and the chessprogramming wiki could not be read directly (blocked), so "Novel" means "not found", per ChessSI's own definition.
- ChessSI's search has no MultiPV and no excluded-move search: a grep of `src/search.*` and `src/uci.*` at the current
  checkout found neither. D, and A's optional second-best-move fork, must add one first, about half a day more.
  Until then, A can rely on a TT reset or node jitter to make branches diverge.
